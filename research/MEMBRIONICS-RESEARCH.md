# Membrionics: research, corrections, and a testable programme

**Research date:** 2026-10-05. Response to [the research programme](prompts/membrionics-deep-research.md), read alongside [the brief](MEMBRIONICS.md), [Beyond the Ten Principles](BEYOND-TEN-PRINCIPLES.md), and [AI Constitutions](../AI-CONSTITUTIONS.md).

**Evidence key:** **E** = documented observation or provision in the cited source; **C** = contested explanation; **I** = this report's inference or proposal. A prescribed rule is evidence of a prescription, not proof of compliance. **P** in the bibliography means relevant passages were inspected; **A** means only an abstract, publisher description, or contents were accessible; **L** means located for follow-up. Page references identify printed pages unless explicitly called PDF pages. This is a critical research synthesis, not a systematic review or a fieldwork report. It covers every named case, but gives greatest weight to cases with inspectable evidence. Unverified particulars and missing outcome evidence remain visible.

## 1. Executive summary

The strongest defensible thesis is: **cultural boundaries regulate the conversion of one kind of access or advantage into another, and their consequences depend on who can revise those conversions.** Entry, exit, memory, sanction, and renewal belong in one account. The claim that boundaries generally matter *more* than cultural contents is not established by these cases. Nor is attention to boundaries a new departure for anthropology: Barth explicitly made boundary maintenance central in 1969. The contribution here is a comparative design vocabulary joining that tradition to AI capabilities. [Barth, introduction, especially pp. 9–16](https://twren.sites.luc.edu/phil389%26elps423/barth.htm).

The most consequential corrections are:

1. **Movement is not exit.** Moving camp, leaving an organization, escaping a reputation network, and retaining a livelihood are different events. The original exit-cost hypothesis conflates them. Oral societies can remember intensely; mobile communities can have courts. Revise H1 around alternative access to indispensable resources and the reach of sanctions.
2. **Grace need not erase history.** Distinguish deletion, restricted access, prohibited reuse, release from an obligation, and restoration of standing. Valencia records verdicts despite oral hearings. Condolence sustains political continuity. Ara Irititja preserves records while controlling sensitive access. Revise H3 from engineered forgetting to engineered limits on the consequences of memory.
3. **Scheduled endings are neither necessary nor sufficient for renewal.** Expiring a mandate can check incumbency but can also destroy expertise or strengthen actors who do not rotate. The annual removal of Black Rock City's structures does not dissolve camps, reputations, placement history, or organizational authority. Drop the literal “compressed Ibn Khaldun cycle”; test selective renewal instead.
4. **Markets do not automatically flatten values.** Trading diasporas and differentiated monetary circuits contradict the binary. Investigate which conversions are authorized: money into political influence, generosity into entitlement, attendance into rights over another person's likeness. A market price is not a scalar measure of a culture's commitments.
5. **The Asgard analogy needs a correction and an update.** The E3 hypothesis is entangle–engulf–endogenize; the brief's extend–entangle–endogenize sequence is its own adaptation. Observations now include dynamic protrusions and crawling, but not a replay of mitochondrial incorporation. Biology supports questions about interdependence and maintenance, not a social imperative to merge.
6. **Burning Man already has a consequential information membrane.** Its 2026 ticket terms restrict participants' uses of event media while granting BMP broad rights over personal attributes; its public guidance emphasizes consent. An AI charter must state whose powers it limits, including the operator's.
7. **Rutt's documented term is “membranics.”** His 2024 conversations with Alexander Bard discuss adaptable permeability and overlapping memberships. The exact spelling “membrionics” and a sole-originator claim were not verified.

Keep H2, H5, and H7 as qualified design propositions; revise H1, H3, H6, and H8; replace H4's universal prediction with a conditional hypothesis. These are research judgments, not estimated causal effects. Detailed evidence follows.

The most useful next experiment is a finite, voluntary, locally processed AI artwork whose **powers expire, sensitive records have separate purposes, and repair remains possible after shutdown**. Evaluate refusal, correction, and maintenance costs before evaluating whether it produces “playa magic.”

## 2. Case dossiers

Each dossier identifies setting; crossing and exit; sanction and return; memory and maintenance; dispute or failure; and relevance. Where a source supplies no return procedure or measured failure, that absence is stated rather than filled with an attractive analogy.

### 2.1 Immediate-return foragers: Woodburn's Hadza comparison

**Setting and mechanism — E.** Woodburn's 1982 synthesis draws especially on his Hadza fieldwork in Tanzania and comparative !Kung material. Direct resource access and flexible association reduce dependence on particular people; mobility is one component. Sharing and restraints on accumulation sustain leveling. This is neither unrestricted entry into any group nor freedom from kinship. **Sanction/return:** association and withdrawal matter, but the article does not establish a general formal amnesty. **Memory/maintenance:** interpersonal knowledge and repeated sharing; absence of bureaucracy does not demonstrate little memory. [“Egalitarian Societies,” pp. 431–439](https://inequality.stanford.edu/sites/default/files/media/_media/pdf/Reference%20Media/Woodburn_1982_History%20of%20Inequality.pdf).

**Limits/failure.** Woodburn explicitly limits many generalizations to adult men (p. 434) and raises tensions between independence and care for dependents. “Immediate return” is not a synonym for all hunter-gathering or all nomadism.

**Relevance — I.** Measure whether a person can obtain shelter, food, care, and affiliation elsewhere. Downloading one's AI data is a weak exit right if the indispensable social network remains behind.

### 2.2 Reverse dominance: Boehm

**Setting and mechanism — C.** This is a cross-cultural explanatory model, not one community. Boehm's 1993 article and 1999 book describe coalitions restraining would-be dominants through criticism, ridicule, disobedience, ostracism, and sometimes lethal action. **Entry/exit:** vary among the underlying cases. **Memory/maintenance:** shared evaluations and coalition mobilization. **Grace:** no common reinstatement protocol follows from the model. [1993 article and published debate, pp. 227–254](https://doi.org/10.1086/204166).

**Disputes/failure.** The article's comments are part of the evidence, not noise to discard. The broad account needs case-level checking of gender, selection, and who can mobilize sanctions. Publisher metadata was accessible; the full debate was not inspected here. Execution itself defeats any inference that leveling necessarily means mild sanctions or reversible exclusion.

**Relevance — I.** Collective control can restrain a leader or empower a punitive majority. An AI that makes coalition sanctions cheap needs protections for the accused, dissenters, and those excluded from the coalition.

### 2.3 Ju/'hoansi leveling: Lee's Christmas ox

**Setting and mechanism — E.** Lee's account concerns /ai/ai and neighboring camps in the 1960s Kalahari, published in 1969. The central incident is the belittling of an **ox bought by the anthropologist**, followed by explanations of analogous treatment of hunters. **Crossing:** an outsider's large gift enters a sharing relationship. **Sanction/return:** teasing corrects the benefactor without preventing the feast. **Memory/maintenance:** participants teach a norm against converting provision into superiority. [Lee, “Eating Christmas in the Kalahari,” accessible reprint, pp. 1–4](https://www.meissinger.com/uploads/3/4/9/1/34919185/lee__1969__eating_christmas.pdf).

**Disputes/failure.** This is a crafted first-person episode, not an experiment demonstrating that status disappears. Lee also recounts remembered violence and disputes over shares: neither harmony nor amnesia follows.

**Relevance — I.** A camp can welcome a major contribution while denying its donor control. Calling this a “direct ancestor” of an invented persona is unwarranted; it is a comparative inspiration. Automating ridicule would remove the relational context that makes the correction intelligible.

### 2.4 Coalitional enforcement: Bingham and Souza

**Setting and mechanism — C.** *Death from a Distance* (2009) is an evolutionary theory, not an ethnographic case. Its inspectable antecedent is Bingham's 1999 paper: remotely inflicted injury can reduce individual enforcement costs when sanctions are coordinated. **Boundary:** cooperative membership; **sanction:** credible collective force; **maintenance:** monitoring and coalition formation; **exit/grace:** not a complete institutional design. [“Human Uniqueness,” pp. 133–169, especially pp. 134–135](https://dfd.63bits.com/upload/blog/Bingham_1999_QRB_74%2C_133-169_2M_version.pdf).

**Disputes/failure.** The strong claim that this is necessary and sufficient for human uniqueness exceeds what the argument establishes. Coalitionary killing also occurs outside humans; that does not refute a specifically human projectile mechanism but blocks a loose uniqueness inference. [Wrangham, 1999](https://doi.org/10.1002/(SICI)1096-8644(1999)110:29+%3C1::AID-AJPA2%3E3.0.CO;2-E). Book-specific passage verification remains open.

**Relevance — I.** Cheaper sanction can enable cooperation **or** predation. Monitoring efficiency alone cannot determine which behavior merits punishment or who may adjudicate it.

### 2.5 Törbel: bounded commons with durable records

**Setting and mechanism — E.** Upper Valais, Switzerland: medieval documentation and twentieth-century ethnography synthesized by Ostrom from Netting. Purchasing land did not automatically buy commons membership. Grazing was constrained by winter fodder capacity; elected officials and labor obligations maintained the alp. **Sanction:** fines, not simply “soft” discipline. **Exit/grace:** no universal reinstatement rule established here. **Memory:** written membership and allocation rules. [Ostrom, ch. 3, pp. 61–65](https://aikon.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/Elinor-Ostrom-Governing-the-Commons_-The-Evolution-of-Institutions-for-Collective-Action-Cambridge-University-Press-2015.pdf).

**Disputes/failure.** This successful case does not establish fairness to outsiders. Ostrom describes overuse elsewhere when appropriation limits or coordination between villages failed; it is incorrect to assign those failures specifically to Törbel.

**Relevance — I.** Resource purchase, use rights, and governing membership can be separated. Long duration with revisable rules is a counterweight to institutional death as the presumed route to adaptation.

### 2.6 Valencian huerta and Tribunal de las Aguas

**Setting and mechanism — E.** Irrigation communities around Valencia, with historically rooted rules and a contemporary court. **Entry:** jurisdiction and water rights are tied to the irrigation system, not general residence. **Sanction/return:** penalties, costs, and compensation resolve infractions without necessarily expelling irrigators. **Memory:** oral hearings coexist with a register of parties, canal, complaint, verdict, and date. **Maintenance:** elected syndics, water guards, ordinances, canal institutions. The court's own [“Operation” account](https://tribunaldelasaguas.org/en/operation/) is explicit about the register; the [2005 official heritage resolution, “Valor jurídico”](https://www.boe.es/diario_boe/txt.php?id=BOE-A-2005-7872) independently describes concise written recording.

**Disputes/failure.** Oral proceedings are not evidence of designed forgetting. Nor is an institution's account of antiquity proof of an unchanged thousand-year constitution. Unavailability of water or destruction of farmland cannot be remedied by quick adjudication alone; this pass did not measure current adjudicative failure rates.

**Relevance — I.** Keep decision records sufficient for accountability without recording every interaction. Data minimization and institutional memory can coexist.

### 2.7 Olson: stable interests and institutional sclerosis

**Setting and mechanism — C.** Olson's *Rise and Decline of Nations* (1982), chs. 2–3, proposes that organized distributional interests accumulate under stable conditions. **Entry/exit:** collective-action advantages let insiders defend privileges. **Sanction:** vetoes, lobbying, and exclusion rather than a single tribunal. **Memory/maintenance:** durable organization. **Grace/reset:** disruption is part of the explanation, not evidence for annual abolition.

**Disputes/failure.** The claimed failure is declining adaptability, but persistence alone cannot identify it. Unger and van Waarden's comparative critique argues that war sometimes strengthened encompassing associations rather than eliminating them. [“Interest associations and economic growth,” 1999](https://doi.org/10.1080/096922999347128). Olson's full text was not inspected; the critique's abstract was. Do not translate a macrohistorical theory into a proven law of camp governance.

**Relevance — I.** Track blocked entrants, concentrated benefits, amendment failures, and unaccountable vetoes. These are more discriminating measures than organizational age or participants' disappointment.

### 2.8 Scott: Zomia, state legibility, and walls

**Setting and mechanism — C.** *The Art of Not Being Governed* (2009), especially chs. 5–6½, concerns upland mainland Southeast Asia and relations with valley states, principally before modern state penetration. Crops, dispersal, and mobility may raise the cost of appropriation. *Seeing Like a State* (1998), chs. 1–2, analyzes administrative simplification. *Against the Grain* (2017), ch. 5, stresses labor capture in early states. These are three scales of argument, not one timeless hill society.

**Exit/memory/sanction.** Flight and reduced legibility may obstruct taxation and conscription; oral histories can also maintain obligations. There is no common grace procedure across “Zomia.”

**Disputes/failure.** Intentional abandonment of writing is particularly weakly evidenced. Michaud's Hmong-focused assessment discusses that difficulty and the presence of states within upland regions. [“What's (written) history for?”, 2017, pp. 6–10](https://www.fss.ulaval.ca/sites/fss.ulaval.ca/files/fss/anthropologie/professeurs/michaud-2017b.pdf). Walls' inward function remains a historical interpretation requiring site-specific evidence, not a general rule.

**Relevance — I.** AI can undo practical obscurity by joining records. Conversely, records may support land claims and recourse. Ask who controls legibility and for what purpose, not whether visibility is inherently domination.

### 2.9 African comparison A: Mandara uplands and lowland raiding states

**Setting and mechanism — E/C.** Dghwede/Gwoza and neighboring Mandara communities, in present Nigeria and Cameroon, in relation to Wandala and the Madagali regime; focus on nineteenth century through Hamman Yaji's 1912–1927 diary. **Crossing/exit:** terrain, defensive arrangements, tribute, and negotiated relations shaped exposure to slave raids; hills did not create isolation. **Memory:** local oral histories and a raider's own records. **Maintenance:** farming communities sustained refuge landscapes. [Müller-Kosack, regional history and linked field sources](https://www.mandaras.info/HistoryOfSlavery.html); [van Beek, diary/oral-history study](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-african-history/article/abs/intensive-slave-raiding-in-the-colonial-interstice-hamman-yaji-and-the-mandara-mountains-north-cameroon-and-northeastern-nigeria/B9291A2920D6D1EDBBDA02AEC251B467).

**Sanction/grace/failure.** Tribute could redirect capture toward other communities; no general restoration procedure for captives is established. Raiding persisted through changing colonial jurisdictions. Chetima's African-authored synthesis and MacEachern's archaeological/ethnohistorical work prevent the romantic inference that refuge populations were outside trade and violence. [Chetima, 2024, “Hamman Yaji at the Colonial Borders”](https://doi.org/10.1093/acrefore/9780190277734.013.890); [MacEachern, 2011, pp. 109–125](https://doi.org/10.5871/bacad/9780197264782.003.0006). The latter two were inspected at abstract/contents level.

**Relevance — I.** A membrane may protect insiders by exporting danger. Audit the neighboring people who bear the cost of one's sanctuary.

### 2.10 African comparison B: Fur cultivators and Baggara pastoralists

**Setting and mechanism — C; limited verification.** Haaland's Darfur fieldwork, published in 1969, examines livelihood and ethnic affiliation before the region's later wars. **Crossing:** some Fur adopting pastoral livelihoods also adopted Baggara affiliation; transactions crossed boundaries that otherwise persisted. **Exit:** a change of livelihood could reorganize membership, not eliminate relationship. **Memory/maintenance:** recognizable practices and mutual classifications. **Sanction/grace:** not verified at passage level in this pass. [“Economic Determinants in Ethnic Processes,” pp. 58–73, bibliographic record](https://www.econbiz.de/Record/economic-determinants-in-ethnic-processes-haaland-gunnar/10002540404).

**Disputes/failure.** Treat this as a selected comparison for further close reading, not established evidence about contemporary Darfur. Economic adaptation alone cannot explain later coercion, racialization, or war. The interpretation may underweight political power; a primary chapter scan and regional scholarship are still needed.

**Relevance — I.** Membership can change through practice and recognition. A database that fixes a person's community label permanently would prohibit the very transition a cultural boundary may allow.

### 2.11 Ibn Khaldun and the annual city

**Setting and mechanism — C.** The *Muqaddimah*, drafted in 1377, reasons from North African and wider Islamic dynastic history. *Asabiyyah* concerns politically effective group solidarity; it is not just nomadic fellowship. Dynastic consolidation, exclusive rule, luxury, and dependence on others change that solidarity. **Entry/exit:** alliance and conquest, not voluntary festival admission. **Memory/maintenance:** lineage claims and institutions; no annual reset. [Rosenthal translation, ch. II, discussion of four generations of prestige; ch. III §12, dynastic lifespan](https://traditionalhikma.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/08/The-Muqaddimah-by-Ibn-Khaldun-Translated-by-Franz-Rosenthal.pdf).

**Disputes/failure.** Three generations of a dynasty and four generations of inherited prestige are different formulations. Neither is a calibrated universal clock. Burning Man's repeated return of organizers and camps is materially unlike replacement through conquest.

**Relevance — I.** Drop the literal compression claim. Study how recurrent building renews cooperation while durable titles, equipment, and access survive. The source does not diagnose the Burning Man organization as stagnant.

### 2.12 Vlax Romani law

**Setting and mechanism — E/C.** Leeson explicitly synthesizes Vlax Roma material from the 1920s–1980s, not all Roma. **Crossing/exit:** physical movement can leave social dependence intact. **Sanction/return:** a kris can impose fines or temporary/permanent exclusion; purity rules can strengthen compliance and incentives to learn newcomers' histories. **Memory/maintenance:** portable reputation and recognized adjudicators. [Leeson, 2013, pp. 275–276, 281–285](https://www.peterleeson.com/gypsies.pdf).

**Disputes/failure.** Leeson's efficiency explanation is an interpretation of earlier accounts. Weyrauch and Bell emphasize autonomous lawmaking; Reisman's response asks about missing dissident and ex-member perspectives. [Weyrauch and Bell, 1993, pp. 323–399](https://openyls.law.yale.edu/bitstream/handle/20.500.13051/8791/22_103YaleLJ323_November1993_.pdf?sequence=2); [Reisman, especially p. 404](https://openyls.law.yale.edu/bitstream/20.500.13051/5272/2/Autonomy_Interdependence_and_Responsibility.pdf). Ron Lee supplies a Romani-authored account; the complete text remains to be inspected. [1997, pp. 345–392](https://doi.org/10.2307/840854). RomArchive's account also identifies gendered access to adjudication. [“Kris”](https://www.romarchive.eu/en/terms/kris/).

**Relevance — I.** This refutes mobility → weak memory, not a carefully defined low-social-exit-cost hypothesis. Distributed enforcement can produce durable exclusion across otherwise independent communities; restorative status needs to travel too.

### 2.13 Aboriginal Australian mourning and restricted knowledge

**Setting and mechanism — E.** Use the specific contemporary Anangu archive Ara Irititja rather than attribute one practice to a continent. Its 2016 policies concern Pitjantjatjara, Yankunytjatjara, and Ngaanyatjarra people. **Crossing:** access depends on cultural authority and appropriate users. **Memory:** recently deceased people's images can be locked; men's and women's materials have separate access arrangements. **Maintenance:** community operators, advisers, software, and funding. **Return:** restrictions can be administered without destroying the archive. [Policies §§1, 4–5, especially pp. 11–12](https://www.irititja.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/2016-Protocols-and-Policies-v2-iphone.pdf).

**Disputes/failure.** The same document describes burdens of short project grants. It documents intended controls, not a security audit. General media warnings do not prove universal name replacement rules or establish that mourning is “forgetting.” Historical name-avoidance practices still need community-specific ethnographic verification.

**Relevance — I.** AI should implement custodians' changing permissions, not infer permission from an archive's existence. Preservation and noncirculation can be complementary.

### 2.14 Yunkaporta's own argument

**Setting/source — P.** *Sand Talk* (2019), opening chapter “The Porcupine, the Paleo-mind and the Grand Design,” publisher excerpt pp. 1–23; this pass did not read the complete book. His self-location in Apalech customary adoption (p. 5) matters: he is not offering a generic account of “nomadic thought.”

**Mechanism.** On pp. 12–17 he rejects externally imposed authenticity categories, distinguishes speaking from knowledge from authority to disclose it, and explains restrictions by relationship, place, and custodial standing. On pp. 19–23 he turns Indigenous pattern-thinking toward contemporary global systems and proposes dialogical work among dissimilar people. Memory resides in practices, objects, relations, and continuing interpretation; a publication is only a partial translation. [Publisher excerpt](https://d2wzqffx6hjwip.cloudfront.net/d72/4af/819/d724af819b3e52a7fa465eebdbfac4513d81a97e390df5a950dc9cb70d51/Yunkaporta_SandTalk_ch1.pdf).

**Limits/relevance — I.** This challenges a research process that exports cultural “content” while discarding authority over its use. Specific sanction and grace procedures cannot be extracted from this opening argument. His [Rutt interview, episode 65](https://www.jimruttshow.com/the-jim-rutt-show-transcripts/transcript-of-episode-65-tyson-yunkaporta-on-indigenous-complexity/) explicitly presents complexity theory as an aligning metaphor. Do not turn that alignment into scientific validation of either cosmology. Later-book protocol formulations need edition-specific close reading.

### 2.15 Haudenosaunee Condolence

**Setting and mechanism — E.** Contemporary Confederacy accounts, including a 2015 Cayuga installation, explain how mourning and succession are joined. **Entry:** clan-mother nomination and successive approvals precede installation. **Sanction:** clan mothers can remove a chief's authority. **Memory/maintenance:** hereditary titles and wampum sustain offices across persons. **Repair:** condolers address mourners' impediments to hearing, seeing, and speaking. [Confederacy newsletter, July 2015, p. 2](https://www.haudenosauneeconfederacy.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/July-2015.pdf); [Confederacy government account](https://www.haudenosauneeconfederacy.com/government/).

**Disputes/failure.** These are community institutional accounts, not proof that every succession is uncontested or a basis for fixing the Great Law's date. No comparative failure rate is established here.

**Relevance — I.** This is a counter-case to repair through amnesia: authority is transferred through remembered continuity. An AI project's stewardship transition should include consent, readiness, and obligations to predecessors and affected people, rather than merely exchanging credentials.

### 2.16 Kwakwaka'wakw potlatch and suppression

**Setting and mechanism — E.** Focus on the 1921 Dan Cranmer potlatch and its aftermath, rather than all Northwest Coast societies. **Crossing:** public witnessing, titles, and ceremonial property connect families. **Sanction:** Canada criminalized ceremonies; participants faced imprisonment and confiscation. **Memory/return:** the return of confiscated regalia and cultural institutions supports continuity. [U'mista, “Our Masks Come Home”](https://umistapotlatch.ca/nos_masques_come_home-our_masks_come_home-eng.php); [Canadian Museum for Human Rights, “Bringing the potlatch home”](https://humanrights.ca/story/bringing-potlatch-home).

**Disputes/failure.** Distinguish the 1884 legislative amendment from the ban's 1885 commencement; it was removed in 1951. Destruction of valuables is not an adequate definition of potlatch, and gift distribution can confirm rank. Confiscation shows externally imposed rupture, not endogenous cultural apoptosis.

**Relevance — I.** Do not use the case to justify burning other people's records or inherited objects. Who may end a practice, and who may demand its preservation, are constitutional questions.

### 2.17 Athenian ostracism

**Setting and mechanism — E/C.** Fifth-century BCE Athens, with a restricted citizen body. A political vote could impose a ten-year removal while preserving property and a route back. It was not conviction for a proved criminal offense. **Memory/maintenance:** publicly counted votes and collective recognition; a recall could shorten absence. [*Athenaion Politeia* 22, especially 22.6–8](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.01.0046%3Achapter%3D22); [modern analysis of precautionary constitutionalism](https://www.law.berkeley.edu/archive/files/Precautionary_Constitutionalism_in_Ancient_Athens.pdf).

**Disputes/failure.** Ancient accounts are retrospective; the institution could serve factional competition as well as restraint of concentrated power. This pass did not resolve disputes about the 6,000-vote threshold, so it is not used.

**Relevance — I.** A sanction can have an ending without erasing public memory. A modern AI-assisted community should not import punishment by popularity; it should make reasons, contestability, and return conditions explicit.

### 2.18 Nuer leopard-skin chief; African comparison C

**Setting and mechanism — E/C.** Evans-Pritchard's 1930s Nuer research in Anglo-Egyptian Sudan, especially *The Nuer* (1940), ch. IV, pp. 150–162. Sanctuary, ritual intervention, negotiation, and cattle compensation mediate homicide disputes. **Exit/return:** resolution depends on relations between groups, not simply leaving a settlement. **Memory:** kinship and compensation claims. **Maintenance:** recognized ritual and lineage relationships. [Primary monograph](https://people.duke.edu/~ldbaker/classes/gradtheory/Evans_Pritchard_E_E_The_Nuer_a_description_of_the_modes_1940.pdf).

**Correction.** Lacking a police force is not lacking power. His later account explicitly treats a leopard-skin chief's curse as consequential within a moral relationship. [“Nuer Curses and Ghostly Vengeance,” 1949, pp. 288–292](https://doi.org/10.2307/1156404). The claim that mediation works *because* the figure has no enforcement power is unsupported.

**Dispute/failure and paired comparison.** Hutchinson and South Sudanese scholar Jok Madut Jok studied Nuer–Dinka relations after the 1991 SPLA split, documenting militarization and changes in restraints on violence. This is a pastoral/agropastoral comparison, **not** nomads versus sedentary cultivators. [Joint research account](https://www.hfg.org/grant_summaries/the-militarization-of-nuer-and-dinka-community-life-a-comparative-field-study-of-the-transformative-impact-of-sudans-unresolved-war/); [Hutchinson, 2000](https://www.bfa.fcnym.unlp.edu.ar/catalogo/doc_num.php?explnum_id=2028).

**Relevance — I.** Mediation depends on resources, legitimacy, and the surrounding conflict. A powerless AI mediator is not thereby neutral or effective.

### 2.19 Cities of refuge

**Setting and mechanism — E as text, not demonstrated implementation.** Numbers 35:9–34 prescribes sanctuary, adjudication, and residence for an unintentional killer until the high priest's death. **Exit:** leaving the city's boundary prematurely exposes the person to the avenger. **Grace:** an external event terminates confinement; it does not require forgetting the killing. **Maintenance:** congregation and priestly/legal authority. [Numbers 35](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Numbers+35&version=KJV).

**Disputes/failure.** The text is not field evidence that this system operated as described in a particular century. Dating, implementation, and comparison with Deuteronomy 19 need specialist treatment. The built-in failure of protection beyond the boundary is explicit.

**Relevance — I.** Sanctuary can protect and constrain at once. A private AI space that makes safety conditional on never leaving or exporting evidence imposes a comparable design dilemma, not a historical equivalence.

### 2.20 Jubilee and Mesopotamian remission

**Setting and mechanism — E/C.** Separate Leviticus 25's normative land-return and release provisions from Old Babylonian royal remission edicts. The biblical text distinguishes insiders and foreign slaves (25:44–46); it is not universal emancipation. Debt remission in Deuteronomy 15 is a related but distinct provision. [Leviticus 25](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Leviticus+25&version=KJV).

**Memory/sanction/return.** Ammi-ṣaduqa's seventeenth-century BCE edict invalidates specified claims and addresses evasion while exempting particular commercial transactions. A surviving document records the **termination of an obligation**; it does not erase historical knowledge. Royal enactment is not a demonstrated fixed periodic schedule. [Finkelstein translation in *Ancient Near Eastern Texts*, edict §§4–8, 20–21](https://e-edu.nbu.bg/pluginfile.php/753301/mod_resource/content/1/AncientNearEasternTexts_Pritchard1969.pdf).

**Disputes/failure.** Jubilee observance is not established here. Exemptions and anticipatory evasion complicate any reset. Graeber is a synthesis, not the primary evidence for these provisions.

**Relevance — I.** Specify which claims expire, whose do not, and how evasion is detected. A “data jubilee” that clears controllers' liability together with participants' histories could benefit the wrong party.

### 2.21 Benedictine enclosure

**Setting and mechanism — E as rule.** Sixth-century Italian monastic prescription. Chapters 53 and 58 combine hospitality with staged admission, repeated instruction, and commitment to stability. Chapter 29 permits a returning brother's reinstatement, with conditions and limits; chapter 61 addresses visiting monks. **Memory/maintenance:** a written rule, offices, and daily routines. [Rule, ch. 29 and linked chapters](https://pressbooks.palni.org/ruleofbenedict/chapter/rb-29/).

**Disputes/failure.** The rule does not demonstrate uniform monastic practice over subsequent centuries. Hospitality does not convey equal governance rights; returning at the lowest rank is a cost, not costless forgiveness. A specific monastery's implementation and abuses require separate evidence.

**Relevance — I.** Guest access, novice participation, full membership, and governing authority can have separate thresholds. Preserve an accessible visitor role rather than demanding maximal consent from everyone who encounters an AI artwork.

### 2.22 Open source: a contemporary boundary case

**Setting/mechanism.** The brief treats forking as exit with shared work. License permissions can enable copying and modification, but neither a project name nor infrastructure, credentials, hosting budgets, or contributors necessarily follows. **Sanction/return:** repositories and community access remain separately governed. **Memory:** public commit histories and issue archives persist. **Maintenance:** release work and institutional succession.

**Verification limit.** No named fork's outcome was studied in this pass. This is a design comparison, not another verified ethnographic dossier. A next-round case should trace an actual fork and allocation of assets rather than cite “open source” as uniformly successful.

**Relevance — I.** AI portability needs an asset-and-authority inventory. Exportable prompts without usable data rights, compute, or relationships may offer nominal rather than effective exit.

### 2.23 Rutt, Bard, and the name

**Verified provenance — E.** In *The Jim Rutt Show* episodes 250 (August 8, 2024) and 252 (August 13, 2024), Rutt and Alexander Bard discuss **membranics**. Rutt explains how a group's internal rules can change smartphone permeability and how memberships overlap without forming a strict hierarchy. Bard places membranes beside memory, learning, and his other philosophical categories. [Episode 250 transcript](https://jimruttshow.blubrry.net/the-jim-rutt-show-transcripts/transcript-of-ep-250-alexander-bard-part-1-process-and-event/); [episode 252 transcript](https://jimruttshow.blubrry.net/the-jim-rutt-show-transcripts/transcript-of-ep-252-alexander-bard-part-2-process-and-event/).

**Limit.** Exact-term searches for “membrionics” did not establish Rutt's use of that spelling, first coinage, or a formal discipline. These transcripts verify usage of the neighboring term, not priority. Audio was not independently checked.

## 3. Hypothesis review

“Strongest” below means strongest among the evidence located in this pass. Several sources show that a proposed universal is false without establishing the opposite universal. A design principle is not an empirical law merely because it is attractive.

| Hypothesis | Supporting evidence | Disconfirming evidence or limit | Judgment and revised wording |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **H1: exit cost determines memory and courts** | Woodburn links independence to alternative associations and access to subsistence. Settled commons need continuing allocation and adjudication. | Vlax social sanctions travel despite mobility; Valencia combines settlement, oral procedure, and records. No cited study measures “little stored memory” as a result of cheap exit. | **Revise; drop the memory prediction.** When people have credible alternatives for indispensable resources and affiliation, incumbents have less leverage. The jurisdiction and portability of sanctions determine whether relocation actually changes dependence. |
| **H2: what follows you determines freedom** | Romani reputation and digital media illustrate consequences beyond a local departure. | Portable credentials, mutual obligations, and evidence can make exit safer. Literal “exit meaningless” ignores partial improvements and variation among destinations. | **Keep, qualify.** Effective exit depends jointly on destination access, retained resources, and the scope, accuracy, contestability, and expiration of portable claims. |
| **H3: grace needs structural forgetting** | Defined limits on exclusion and claims can make return credible: ostracism, Benedict's return provisions, remission. | Condolence preserves offices and history; Ara Irititja preserves restricted records; Valencia records outcomes. These are not variants of literal forgetting. | **Revise.** Durable grace requires an enforceable change in what the past authorizes: sometimes deletion, sometimes restricted use, terminated claims, or restored standing with retained evidence. |
| **H4: scheduled endings prevent sclerosis** | Sunset provisions can force review; restrictions on tenure can open positions. | Enduring commons revise rules without ending the institution. Term limits can weaken expertise; sunsets can be perfunctory. | **H6: ordeal selects commitment** | Staged monastic entry and costly-commitment studies suggest that visible sacrifices can correlate with durable cooperation. | Money, time, disability, care duties, and physical capacity change the price of the same ordeal. | **H8: market crossing erodes gradients** | Commoditization can permit new conversions and shift control over culturally restricted goods. | Diasporic trade and Zelizer's circuits show intense commerce alongside durable distinctions. ### 3.1 Exit as a vector, with explicit alternatives

Use an **exit profile**, not one number:

`(physical safety, livelihood, housing, care, affiliation, credentials, assets, data, exposure to sanctions)`.

For each dimension record what is lost, retained, portable, or refused by the destination. A camper switching tents and a person leaving a kin network are not comparable observations simply because both move. Identify the sanctioning network separately from residence and ethnicity. This avoids protecting H1 by redefining every counter-case as “really costly exit.”

**Proposed test — I.** Compare camp changes within Black Rock City, movement to a regional event, and departure from Burning Man networks. Record actual access at the destination, reciprocal affiliations, reputation-sharing procedures, and reasons for rejection. If high-quality alternatives do not reduce gatekeepers' leverage, after accounting for sanctions that travel, the revised hypothesis is weakened. Include people who departed and did not return; sampling only current members would select for survivors.

### 3.2 What the sunset and term-limit evidence actually says

Sobel and Dove's cross-state study associates sunset provisions with lower regulatory levels; its outcome is not organizational creativity or distributive fairness. [2016, pp. 446–477](https://doi.org/10.1177/1091142114557723). A newer study distinguishes agency and rule sunsets and reports context-sensitive results rather than a uniform effect. [“An iridescent sunset,” 2025, especially §6.4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11149-025-09498-5). OECD's own review describes how excessive review loads can make sunset scrutiny superficial. [*Reviewing the Stock of Regulation*, “Broad approaches to reviews”](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/reviewing-the-stock-of-regulation_1a8f33bc-en/full-report/component-8.html).

Term limits likewise require separated outcomes. Moncrief and Thompson's lobbyist survey reports perceived shifts toward executives, agencies, and interest groups; it is evidence of perceptions, not a clean causal estimate. [2001](https://doi.org/10.1177/153244000100100404). Garlick's California study distinguishes staffing cuts from term limits and does **not** find that term-limited legislators became more likely to introduce or pass externally authored bills. [2025, “Legislative capacity limits interest group influence”](https://doi.org/10.1111/lsq.12478). The counterargument cannot honestly be simplified into “term limits always empower lobbyists.”

**Design inference.** Separate four interventions: rotating people, expiring powers, reviewing rules, and closing organizations. Compare them against a defined failure such as incumbent veto, delayed appeal, or inaccessible entry. Preserve training, archives of institutional decisions, and service obligations while making authority contestable. A review date without time, information, and power to say no is weak renewal.

### 3.3 The annual rebuild is selective, not total

The relevant unit is a set of linked durations:

| What may end annually | What can persist | Testable question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A physical installation or city layout | Designs, equipment, donors, artists' reputations | Does rebuilding admit new creators or reproduce incumbent advantages? |
| A particular year's camp placement | Camp organizations, standing, ticket relationships | How often do newcomers gain access, and can old standing be challenged? |
| A participant's event experience | Images, contacts, exclusion decisions, skills | Which consequences survive without renewed authorization? |
| A volunteer shift or seasonal role | Professional staff, accumulated competence, informal influence | Does rotation redistribute decisions or only the visible labor? |

These distinctions follow from the documented placement and cleanup processes in §6. **There is no causal evidence here that the annual burn prevents sclerosis, or that the permanent organization is stagnant.** A viable longitudinal test needs measures of leadership concentration, failed amendments, entrant access, retention of experienced maintainers, and the unequal labor costs of rebuilding. Compare rotating and nonrotating powers within the same organization before attempting a historical grand cycle.

## 4. Value gradients: from binary metaphor to a model of permitted conversion

### 4.1 What the literature changes

**Kopytoff:** commoditization is a changing classification, not an object's intrinsically history-free condition. Objects may enter and leave exchangeability, and observers may disagree about their status. Provenance can itself increase market value. The useful question is who may change an object's exchange status. [1986, ch. 2, pp. 64–92](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511819582.004).

**Mauss and Hyde:** Mauss analyzes obligations to give, accept, and reciprocate, including competitive and hierarchical relations; this is not identical to Burning Man's norm against an expected equivalent return. [*The Gift*, introduction and chs. I–II](https://www.ssoar.info/ssoar/bitstream/document/89718/1/ssoar-1954-mauss-The_gift_forms_and_functions.pdf). Hyde explicitly allows an artwork's market circulation and its reception as a gift to coexist. His argument should not be recruited as proof that payment erases meaningful relation. [*The Gift*, introduction, pp. xxxii–xxxiii in the supplied edition](https://lewishyde.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/GIFT-Introduction-1.pdf).

**Bohannan and Walzer:** Tiv exchange spheres differentiated subsistence, prestige, and claims over persons; cross-sphere conversion was morally consequential, not simply impossible. This outsider ethnography must not turn control over women into an attractive design principle. [Bohannan, 1955, pp. 60–70](https://www.jstor.org/stable/665788). Walzer's blocked exchanges are a normative account of preventing dominance between spheres, not an ethnographic law that any traditional prohibition is just. His *Spheres of Justice* (1983), ch. 4, needs a complete close reading; a useful dispute is Waldron's criticism of its treatment of money and exchangeability. [“Money and Complex Equality,” 1995](https://academic.oup.com/book/1891/chapter-abstract/141653334).

**Polanyi and Zelizer:** Polanyi's account of labor, land, and money as fictitious commodities identifies the political construction and consequences of self-regulating markets. It does not show that every transaction destroys embeddedness. [*The Great Transformation*, ch. 6](https://www.uvm.edu/~gflomenh/courses/CDAE253/readings/Polanyi-fictcommod.pdf). Zelizer's relational work goes further: people actively match transactions and payment media to relationships, creating distinctions inside monetary life. [2012 author manuscript](https://csso.princeton.edu/document/49). Thus the report rejects “market or meaningful relation” as the organizing binary.

**Fiske:** communal sharing, authority ranking, equality matching, and market pricing are models for coordinating relationships; they can combine. “Market pricing” means proportional relations and is broader than cash. The vocabulary identifies a change in relational rules without declaring one universal scale of human value. [1992, pp. 689–723](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.99.4.689).

**Malinowski:** the early twentieth-century Massim Kula connects partners through differentiated valuables, directions of circulation, and enduring relationships; ordinary trade accompanies it. It is a directed network, not simply a smooth landscape of neighboring communities sharing almost all commitments. [*Argonauts of the Western Pacific*, chs. III and XIV](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/55822). Its account requires supplementation on women's wealth and colonial conditions before generalizing the male-centered picture.

### 4.2 Counter-cases: commerce can support distinction

**Hausa traders in Sabo, Ibadan.** Cohen's 1962–1963 fieldwork and historical reconstruction link long-distance cattle/kola trade to lodging and brokerage, political organization, and religious affiliation. Membership and trust are maintained while goods travel widely. [*Custom and Politics in Urban Africa*, especially chs. 3–6; publisher description and contents](https://www.routledge.com/Custom-and-Politics-in-Urban-Africa-A-Study-of-Hausa-Migrants-in-Yoruba-Towns/Cohen/p/book/9780415329811). The full ethnography was not inspected here. Its functional explanation may overstate instrumental ethnic unity, but the counter-case is enough to reject the necessity of market isolation. It also shows that internal solidarity can protect commercial privilege rather than equality.

**Eleventh-century Maghribi traders.** Greif interprets Geniza correspondence as evidence of multilateral reputation enforcement among Mediterranean Jewish merchants. Edwards and Ogilvie dispute the closed-coalition account and emphasize courts, bilateral relations, and associations with non-Maghribis; Greif explicitly contests their reading. [Greif, 1989, pp. 857–882](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/identifier/S0022050700009475/type/journal_article); [Edwards and Ogilvie, 2012, pp. 421–444](https://sheilaghogilvie.com/wp-content/uploads/publications/Edwards-Ogilvie-2012-Maghribi-traders-EHR.pdf); [Greif's reply, pp. 445–469](https://people.bu.edu/chamley/HSFref/GreifEHR12.pdf). Preserve the disagreement: the evidence supports extensive commerce through social institutions, but the precise mixture of legal and reputational enforcement is disputed.

**Merchant guilds.** Ogilvie's *Institutions and European Trade* (2011) challenges efficiency accounts of guild privileges, including their alleged informational advantages. [Chapter 9, “Information”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/institutions-and-european-trade/information/FA072E87DA9531257801CE833B6DF7FC). Guilds are candidates for both boundary maintenance and entrenched exclusion. A close reading of a particular guild is still needed; “merchant guild” should not stand in for one homogeneous outcome.

“Middleman minority” is a comparative category, not a diagnosis of an ethnic character. Investigate particular host laws, insecurity, brokerage, credit, marriage, language, and institutions. Strong internal boundaries can coexist with dependence on outsiders and exposure to persecution.

### 4.3 Is arbitrage a kind of cultural diffusion?

**Narrowly defensible:** when a good is transferable and treated as equivalent across places, profitable resale tends to narrow price differences, subject to transport, finance, risk, and enforcement. Even financial arbitrage is constrained. [Shleifer and Vishny, 1997, pp. 35–55](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1997.tb03807.x).

**Not established:** price equalization implies convergence of moral commitments. A common unit of payment does not imply common judgments about sacred obligations, political legitimacy, or admissible uses. Competitive differentiation may reward stronger differences. Economic arbitrage, norm imitation, coercion, and cultural appropriation therefore require different mechanisms.

The more promising analogy is **unauthorized conversion**: gain standing in one context, then spend it in another where it was not meant to confer authority. Examples for investigation include a donor claiming creative control, a participant selling a confidential encounter, or a vendor training on material supplied only to provide a local service. “Arbitrage” is useful only after specifying the actor, transferable right, two rule systems, expected gain, and externalized cost.

Formal cultural models offer a related but separate mechanism. Axelrod represents culture with multiple features; homophilous interaction and influence can produce local convergence with persistent global differences. The model is not a theory of markets or an empirical proof of protected communities. [1997, pp. 203–226, model specification and results](https://web.mit.edu/curhan/www/docs/Articles/15341_Readings/Culture_and_Identity/Axelrod-1997.pdf). This pass found **no validated formal model identifying market arbitrage with erosion of multidimensional cultural commitments**. That negative result is limited to this search, not a claim of exhaustive absence.

### 4.4 A nonscalar map

**Proposal — I.** Represent a community at time *t* by a **profile of practices and contested commitments**, with distributions across members. Do not assign one cultural quality score. Use dimensions such as image circulation, privacy of dissent, reciprocity, openness to guests, tolerance of variation, and stewardship of shared resources. Allow “unknown” and recorded disagreement.

For communities *i* and *j*, record a separate directed edge for each crossing type *k*:

`E(i,j,k) = {permission, decision-maker, conditions, reciprocal duties, retained claims, appeal, expiry}`.

Then record conversions separately: **which normative relation changes, between whom, through whose authorized act?** A camp may admit a visitor while refusing photography; accept a donation without a board seat; share a tool without its maintenance burden transferring; share public documentation without disclosing participant records. Similar stated values do not guarantee traversable edges. Community-to-community edges summarize relations whose actual holders may be individuals, offices, or collectives; identify them rather than assuming a community speaks with one voice.

| Example community pair | Shared commitments | Difference to preserve | Crossing rule to investigate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Two neighboring camps | Mutual aid and event participation | One permits photographs; one does not | Visitors change recording practice on crossing; consent does not automatically travel |
| A camp and a regional event | Shared principles and volunteering | Local governance and land obligations | Skills may transfer; placement entitlements do not automatically transfer |
| An artwork and an archive | Creative documentation | Immediate encounter versus long retention | Deposit needs a separate scope and authority; collection does not imply training permission |

These examples are proposed mappings, not claims about named camps. Visualization should use a layered directed graph, annotated profiles, and uncertain edges. A weighted distance may be useful for one stated analytic purpose, but publish the dimensions and weights and never treat it as the community's intrinsic worth. Avoid using model embeddings as culturally authoritative distances: they reproduce the model's corpus and categories.

#### Hohfeld: specifying the relations at a crossing

Hohfeld's analysis distinguishes four relations commonly compressed into “having a right.” It applies beyond property, and does not itself assume that the relations are separable commodities. The following cultural examples are **proposals — I**, not assertions of enforceable legal rights. [Hohfeld, “Some Fundamental Legal Conceptions as Applied in Judicial Reasoning” (1913), discussion of jural correlatives](https://persweb.wabash.edu/facstaff/helmang/phi213-1314S/phi213-txtbrwsr/Hohfeld/Hohfeld1913.html).

| Relation | Correlative position | Proposed cultural example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Claim** | Another party has a duty | A participant has a claim that a photographer respect an agreed restriction |
| **Privilege / liberty** | Another party lacks a claim forbidding the action | A guest may participate; this alone creates no entitlement to assistance |
| **Power** | Another party is subject to a change in their normative position | An authorized steward can admit someone to a role |
| **Immunity** | Another party lacks power to make that change | A community's governing arrangements cannot be altered by a sponsor's unilateral donation |

Permission to act, entitlement to another's action, and authority to change a relation require separate entries. “Liability,” the correlative of power, means susceptibility to normative change here, not necessarily blame or financial responsibility. For each incident, record its holder, counterpart, action or subject matter, normative source, conditions, and means of challenge. A social expectation, an organizational rule, and a legal entitlement may overlap or conflict; the map should preserve those differences.

Commons scholarship provides a direct bridge. Schlager and Ostrom distinguish access, withdrawal, management, exclusion, and alienation, showing how resource users can hold substantial rights without authority to sell them. These categories describe the **content** of resource arrangements; Hohfeld helps distinguish the **kind of relation** involved. They are complementary rather than interchangeable classifications. [Schlager and Ostrom, “Property-Rights Regimes and Natural Resources” (1992), especially pp. 250–252](https://faculty.sites.iastate.edu/tesfatsi/archive/tesfatsi/PropertyRightsRegimesNaturalResources.SchlagerOstrom1992.pdf).

#### What a bundle description can leave unexplained

**Diagnostic proposal — I.** Difficulty fitting a “bundle of rights” is a reason to investigate, not proof that a practice transcends transactional description. Distinguish at least four possibilities:

- **Tacit or contested relations:** participants disagree about who owes what. Specification may clarify the disagreement without settling it.
- **Inseparable or nontransferable incidents:** authority comes with stewardship duties that cannot be detached or sold. Hohfeld can describe this; the bundle metaphor misleads if it implies independently tradable components.
- **Meaning beyond entitlements:** a friendship or shared practice may generate claims without its meaning being exhausted by those claims. A normative inventory need not be a complete account of the relationship.
- **Formation through participation:** the relationship changes a person's capacities, commitments, or understanding. Allocating its permissions does not by itself produce those changes.

This last possibility adds a second layer to the map: **the processes through which people become capable of occupying its relations**. Consider a hypothetical communal craft apprenticeship. Supervised access, practice, responsibility, and recognition may eventually produce someone trusted to teach. Transferring the final permissions to a newcomer would not transfer the competence, trust, or orientation that makes the role work.

Separate **transformation of standing** from **transformation of the person**. An appointment or ceremony can change recognized membership or authority: Hohfeld's concept of power can describe that normative event. Learning to perceive, judge, or care differently is another process. Rights can govern its conditions without explaining or producing it. Callard's account of aspiration as learning to appreciate values one does not yet fully grasp offers a relevant philosophical framework. The publisher's abstract and chapter description were inspected, not the complete book; this is a conceptual connection, not evidence about a particular cultural practice. [Callard, *Aspiration: The Agency of Becoming* (2018), introduction and ch. 1](https://academic.oup.com/book/4936).

For each proposed crossing, ask what requires practice, recognition, changed commitments, or an ongoing relationship; who judges readiness; and how that judgment can be challenged. Do not reduce this to a scalar “transformation score.” Distinguish acquiring a capability, learning to value something, and satisfying a gatekeeper: they need not coincide.

> **If we transferred the stated entitlements while removing the formative relationship, would the practice still work—and would participants recognize it as the same practice?**

A “no” identifies something a purely allocative account leaves unexplained. It does not invalidate Hohfeld or automatically justify exclusion. Claims that someone “has not been transformed” can conceal arbitrary gatekeeping; retain evidence of competence, disagreement, and paths of appeal. Formation may also occur outside the institution claiming authority to certify it.

**Membrane and AI implication — I.** A membrane may govern becoming as well as crossing. Authorizing an AI system to perform a task differs from recognizing it as occupying a role grounded in experience, responsibility, and relationships. Producing an expected output does not by itself establish the latter. The research question is which role requirements can be demonstrated through performance and which depend on continuing relationships or responsibilities that a permission grant alone cannot supply.

**Candidate paragraph for the brief:** A value gradient is a pattern of partial continuity across distinct communities, sustained by relationships and negotiated crossings. It need not be geographically smooth, morally hierarchical, or opposed to money. What protects difference is the ability to govern conversions between kinds of value and authority. Specify who holds claims, liberties, powers, and immunities, then ask how participants become capable of occupying those relations. Some crossings allocate access; others involve learning, recognition, or changed commitments. A map of entitlements should preserve the formative relationships that make those entitlements meaningful.

## 5. Analogy audit: biology and physics

### 5.1 Claim-by-claim findings

| Brief's claim | Audit | Transferable mechanism or careful use |

**Biological sources and exact scope:** Mitchell's [1961 proposal, pp. 144–148](https://doi.org/10.1038/191144a0) and Mitchell–Moyle's [1965 experimental report](https://doi.org/10.1038/2081205a0); Lane–Martin's [2010 hypothesis, pp. 929–934](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature09486), contrasted with Lynch–Marinov's [2015 analysis, pp. 15690–15695](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1514974112); Varela–Maturana–Uribe's [1974 formal account, pp. 187–196](https://doi.org/10.1016/0303-2647(74)90031-8); the Nomenclature Committee's [2018 recommendations, sections on intrinsic apoptosis, regulated necrosis, and immunogenic death](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5864239/). Matzinger's [2002 danger-model viewpoint](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1071059) is an influential challenge to the self/nonself framing, not a replacement consensus established by this report.

### 5.2 What newer Asgard work adds

Imachi et al. isolated *“Candidatus Prometheoarchaeum syntrophicum”* MK-D1 from deep marine sediment. Syntrophic growth and elaborate surface protrusions supported their **entangle–engulf–endogenize** scenario; they did not observe a mitochondrion being acquired. [2020, pp. 519–525, E3 model](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-019-1916-6). The brief's “extend–entangle–endogenize” is a social rewrite that removes the hypothesized engulfment step. Label it accordingly.

Rodrigues-Oliveira et al. described *“Candidatus Lokiarchaeum ossiferum”*, visualizing an actin cytoskeleton in complex cellular architecture. That strengthens the morphological evidence but does not settle the sequence of eukaryogenesis. [Published online 2022; *Nature* 613, 332–339 (2023), microscopy and discussion](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-022-05550-y).

Radler et al., **published September 30, 2026**, report live-cell imaging of Lokiarchaea and Hodarchaea, growing/retracting protrusions and crawling on glass; selected actin inhibitors arrested those dynamics. This moves the evidence beyond static images. It still does not show deliberate “commons investment,” environmental conditioning in the social sense, or engulfment of the mitochondrial ancestor. [Abstract and main text](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11063-9). Alternative eukaryogenesis scenarios remain live; a useful comparison is the [revisited syntrophy hypothesis, 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41564-020-0710-4).

**Social inference:** dependency, adjacency, and incorporation are distinct states. Test whether shared infrastructure increases each partner's options or makes one indispensable. Consent and exit rights are human additions to the analogy, not biological discoveries.

### 5.3 Prigogine: an accounting discipline, not an optimization rule

In physical thermodynamics, an open system's entropy change is separated into internal production and exchange with its environment. A maintained local pattern need not violate the second law. Prigogine's lecture concerns the emergence and stability of nonequilibrium structures, not a universal claim that maximizing throughput maximizes complexity. [1977 Nobel lecture, “Time, Structure and Fluctuations”](https://www.nobelprize.org/uploads/2018/06/prigogine-lecture.pdf).

For a social application, list resource inflows, work, maintenance, outputs, and exported burdens. Fuel use and heat are physical quantities; fatigue, legitimacy, money, and attention are not interchangeable energy units. No physical theorem follows from adding them together. Spin glasses should not be presented as the standard exemplar of actively maintained dissipative order. The user's remembered “islands of order” quotation was not verified verbatim.

**Proposed study — I.** Track the labor and resources required to sustain a camp's consent boundary, including training and complaint handling after the event. Test whether underfunded maintenance predicts observable failures. That is an institutional maintenance model inspired by an open-system question; it does not require claiming that a culture obeys a new entropy law.

### 5.4 Assembly Theory: define the object before measuring it

Sharma et al. combine assembly pathways and copy numbers in a proposed account of selection. The shortest allowed construction pathway is not automatically the actual historical pathway. [2023, pp. 321–328](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06600-9). Critiques dispute novelty and explanatory power, including comparisons with compression; the proponents contest those equivalences. [Abrahão et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41540-024-00403-y); [proponents' response, preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12452). These disagreements remain unresolved here.

A social operationalization would have to specify: the object (a procedure? text? performance?), basic units, permissible combinations, reuse, copying, and what counts as another instance. Different grammars may give different indices. Benchmark any result against simpler descriptions such as dependency depth, training hours, or replacement time. High assembly is not necessarily good; a harmful bureaucracy may be difficult to reconstruct. Until those tests exist, use the analogy to ask about **loss of capability**, not to rank cultures or justify preserving every institution.

### 6.2 The image-rights asymmetry is concrete

The **2026 ticket terms** distinguish participant event content, uses by BMP, and representation for enforcement. Section 5 includes restrictions on stock-photography services and assigns joint copyright for enforcing unauthorized distribution. Section 6 acknowledges possible capture/dissemination without further consent and grants BMP use of personal attributes broadly. Section 7 appoints BMP to protect specified rights but disclaims an obligation to act. These are the contract's assertions; this report does not decide their enforceability in any particular dispute. [Terms §§5–7](https://burningman.org/black-rock-city/ticketing-information/ticket-terms-and-conditions/).

The public media guidance asks photographers to seek permission and describes consent-respecting use. That guidance and the broad organizational grant should not be collapsed into a single “everyone controls their image” assurance. [Media guidance](https://burningman.org/news/press-media/press-rights-responsibilities/). The exact string “artificial” was absent from the ticket page inspected; this establishes neither unrestricted AI permission nor the absence of other relevant policies.

**Constitutional implication — I.** An AI installation needs permission categories for capture, inference, display, export, training, and autonomous action, plus separate limits on operators. Being an attendee, accepting ticket terms, or possessing an image must not be treated as sufficient evidence of consent to all six.

**2009–2011, image control.** EFF challenged the earlier copyright/takedown structure and participated in discussions. Its 2011 account acknowledges improvements while retaining objections to takedown powers. This is primary evidence of a participant in the dispute, not an impartial legal ruling. **Connectivity.** The 2018 Journal argument against smartphone-mediated distraction and the 2022 defense of remote participation show an explicit internal tension. ## 7. AI implications: enforceable boundaries and institutional duties

### 7.1 All thirteen axes, with practical limits

These are **proposed design requirements**, not descriptions of systems currently used by BMP. Technical enforcement always has a scope: device, process, account, service, or cooperative federation. A promise extending beyond that scope needs a named institution and remedy.

| Axis | AI capability changing it | Technically enforceable within a stated system | Institutional duty or unresolved limit | Precedent |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

| **Direction** | Silent uploads, telemetry, tool calls, publication | Deny network egress by default; allowlisted endpoints; separate upload and display permissions | Providers and recipients must honor agreed purposes; a local screen can still be photographed | Purpose limitation; access-control policies |

**Unlinkability needs a threat model.** Pairwise pseudonyms and separate identifiers can reduce routine linkage; faces, voices, timing, location, and social graphs can defeat it. On-device inference reduces transmission only if logging, backups, updates, and operator access are controlled. Encryption protects access under defined assumptions; it does not make an authorized recipient forget.

### 7.2 Precedents and their limits

**Data protection.** GDPR purpose limitation, minimization, and storage limitation are useful precedents; arts. 17–19 distinguish erasure, restriction, and notification. Erasure has exceptions, including legal claims. Article 22 concerns specified solely automated decisions with legal or similarly significant effects; it is not a universal ban on automation. Applicability to a Nevada event or a particular operator needs its own analysis. This report uses the law as a design precedent rather than asserting jurisdiction. [Official GDPR text, arts. 3, 5, 17–19, 22, 25](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32016R0679).

**Contextual integrity.** Nissenbaum explains why information observable in public is not therefore appropriate for unrestricted collection and redistribution. Analyze sender, recipient, subject, information type, and transmission conditions. Local norms still require ethical evaluation; oppressive customs do not become justified merely by being customary. [2004, especially the contextual-integrity discussion, pp. 137–143](https://digitalcommons.law.uw.edu/wlr/vol79/iss1/10/).

**Deletion and unlearning.** NIST SP 800-88r2 concerns sanitizing media under specified conditions, including cryptographic erasure; it does not certify disappearance of uncontrolled copies. [2025 standard](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/88/r2/final). Bourtoule et al.'s SISA architecture reduces retraining scope through partitioning; it is not a universal operation for extracting one person's influence from arbitrary deployed models. [Author implementation and paper reference](https://github.com/cleverhans-lab/machine-unlearning). Thudi et al. argue for auditable algorithmic definitions: output behavior alone cannot establish how training data were used. [USENIX Security 2022](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity22/presentation/thudi).

**Collective authority.** Local Contexts TK Labels communicate community-specific circulation conditions, including secret/sacred material. They are meaningful governance metadata, not a cryptographic lock or an automatic replacement for legal rights. [TK Labels](https://localcontexts.org/labels/traditional-knowledge-labels/). A permissively accessible file may still be subject to cultural responsibilities.

**Platform governance.** Jhaver, Bruckman, and Gilbert study how explanations of removed Reddit content relate to subsequent behavior. This supports investigating understandable reasons, but does not establish that any explanation makes sanctions just. [2019 author record and paper](https://shagunjhaver.com/research/articles/jhaver-2019-transparency/). Appeals require someone able to reverse the action and its downstream effects.

### 7.3 Design principles and a reviewable experiment

1. **Authorize conversions separately.** Capture, inference, display, export, training, and acting for someone are distinct grants. A creative contribution does not confer control over another participant's identity.
2. **Bound operator power.** Apply retention, purpose, and publication limits to administrators and contractors; identify any exceptional access and its reviewer.
3. **Separate creative memory from recourse evidence.** Keep a short-lived interaction store, a voluntarily deposited archive, and a restricted complaint store with distinct access and retention rules. Do not silently copy everything into “safety” storage.
4. **Make exit feasible.** Offer a usable non-AI route, clear physical boundaries, and refusal without loss of unrelated camp benefits. Track what follows the person and which third parties must be notified of corrections.
5. **Make restoration explicit.** A reversed decision should update downstream eligibility; a completed sanction should not remain active under a renamed score.
6. **End powers before ending care.** Shut down sensors, outbound actions, and delegated credentials at the agreed time while preserving the minimum resourced mechanism for complaints and transfers of duty.
7. **Keep affiliation plural.** Independent communities may cooperate without pooled personal histories or a universal reputation score. Share narrowly justified eligibility decisions only when authorized, accurate, and contestable.

**Proposed pilot — I, not a deployment.** A participant-operated improvisational artwork accepts only voluntarily submitted text in a physically marked area. It processes locally, has no ambient camera or microphone, and cannot publish or contact anyone. A separate affirmative action is required to save a chosen output. The event charter specifies a short interaction-retention period, a finite operating period, a named steward, accessible refusal, and a restricted complaint pathway after closure. Exact retention durations should be chosen with participants and the real technical stack, not invented as universal standards.

**Acceptance criteria:** inspect network traffic and logs; test expiry across every controlled store; verify shutdown of background jobs and credentials; test refusal and correction with participants; rehearse steward absence and a complaint after teardown. Report failures and maintenance hours. Success is demonstrated control of scope plus a worthwhile voluntary creative experience—not a model's claim that it “forgot,” and not an aggregate community score.

The literature remains weighted toward external observers, particularly for Hadza, Ju/'hoansi, Tiv, and historical Nuer accounts. Community-authored materials were located for Haudenosaunee and Kwakwaka'wakw institutions; Anangu governance and Yunkaporta materially shape the argument. Chetima and Jok broaden the African literature; Ronald Lee is identified but not falsely presented as fully read. The next round should improve this imbalance rather than merely add more outsider citations.

### 9.2 A falsifiable programme, not a membrane theory of everything

**Unit of analysis:** a specified crossing or conversion, governed by identifiable actors, over a stated interval. “A culture” is too coarse to be the sole unit. Record people who belong to several groups and those whom every group refuses.

**Outcomes:** preserve several separate outcomes—creative opportunity, newcomer access, safety, concentrated power, successful repair, retention of capability, and maintenance burden. A boundary can succeed on one and fail on another. Neither survival nor distinctiveness is sufficient evidence of justice.

**Comparison strategy:** begin with two or three camps or projects differing in a specific rule. Use policy histories and voluntary interviews to distinguish intention, practice, and consequence. Look for changed rules, failed appeals, uncompleted exits, and obligations shifted to outsiders. Do not gather sensitive personal histories merely to prove the importance of opacity.

**Rival explanations:** wealth, geography, prior friendships, labor availability, leadership, policing, and survivorship. The membrane vocabulary earns its place only if its mechanisms add explanatory precision beyond these factors.

**Stopping rule:** retire an analogy when it supplies no measurable mechanism or useful distinction. Keep biological analogies for generating questions; retain the social conclusions only where social evidence supports them. The constructive result of this round is a narrower and more operational thesis: **protect meaningful differences by governing crossings and conversions, make power contestable, and preserve the capacity to repair what crosses badly.**
