# Extending Beyond the Ten Principles of Burning Man

This research project supplies the cultural and philosophical foundation for
the broader work tracked in [`ROADMAP.md`](../ROADMAP.md). Its initial synthesis
and sources are preserved below; the AI material remains an input to the new
[`AI-CONSTITUTIONS.md`](../AI-CONSTITUTIONS.md) project, rather than an adopted
constitution. A short [summary](../BEYOND-TEN-PRINCIPLES-SUMMARY.md) sits at
the top level of the repository, and the [overview](../README.md) builds its
pillars on this model.

## Research Goal

Deconstruct Burning Man's Ten Principles, identify the social and philosophical
mechanisms that produce "playa magic," and evolve those mechanisms into a
portable framework for present and future communities composed of humans,
algorithmic media, AI systems, and other emerging cybernetic agents.

The objective is not to modernize the wording for its own sake, produce an
eleventh-principle checklist, or preserve Burning Man as doctrine. It is to
preserve what is most socially generative in the culture while separating:

- values from norms;
- ideals from enforceable duties;
- constitutive practices from desired effects;
- table stakes from aspirations;
- stated ethos from actual governance;
- local playa adaptations from principles that can generalize;
- human practices from assumptions invalidated by AI and cybernetic mediation.

The intended result is a compact, usable account of the conditions under which
people cease to be spectators and consumers, become co-creators and citizens,
and generate trust, serendipity, belonging, experimentation, and shared meaning.

## Originating Questions

1. What kind of thing is each of the Ten Principles: value, virtue, norm,
   practice, permission, prohibition, duty, environmental constraint,
   governance rule, or phenomenological aspiration?
2. Which are prerequisites for the social field, which are activities within
   it, and which are hoped-for consequences?
3. Which principles are institutionally enforced, informally policed, merely
   celebrated, or routinely contradicted?
4. What causal mechanisms, rather than slogans, produce the openness,
   generosity, participation, and serendipity commonly called "playa magic"?
5. How much of that effect depends on the desert, ordeal, pilgrimage, bounded
   duration, ritual, ephemerality, and city design rather than the stated
   Principles?
6. What can travel into ordinary and durable social life without turning into
   lifestyle branding, bureaucracy, or a pale simulation of a temporary event?
7. How must the framework change when nonhuman systems can observe, remember,
   recommend, generate, transact, persuade, coordinate, and act?
8. In mixed human-machine systems, who designs the feedback loops, who can see
   and alter them, whose purposes do they serve, and who remains accountable?

## Orientation

This inquiry treats the Ten Principles as evidence from a successful but
contradictory social experiment, not as axioms beyond criticism. It will attend
to lived behavior and institutional consequences as closely as to official
language. The analysis should remain capable of saying both that an element is
socially powerful and that its benefits are distributed unequally or sustained
by hidden labor, capital, ecological cost, or coercion.

Material in [*The Unmoored Self*](https://github.com/pzwang/unmoored-self) may inform this project,
especially its accounts of embodied co-presence, de-simulation, relational
selfhood, attention, norms, obligation, conviviality, and the medium as an
active participant. That repository is a read-only source for this project; no
project output will be written there.

## Working Standard

Every proposed principle or dimension should eventually be stated at several
levels:

| Level | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Value | What is worth protecting or cultivating? |
| Practice | What do participants repeatedly do? |
| Affordance | What does the environment make easy, hard, visible, or impossible? |
| Norm | What behavior do participants expect from one another? |
| Governance | Who interprets the norm, detects violations, and applies consequences? |
| Feedback | What signals reveal whether the social field is becoming healthier or degrading? |
| Failure mode | How can the formulation be gamed, captured, ritualized, or Goodharted? |
| Cybernetic extension | How does it apply when adaptive machine agents participate in the loop? |

No elegant formulation counts as progress unless it changes how a space is
designed, inhabited, governed, or evaluated.

### Historical View

The Ten Principles were not written in the late 1980s or 1990s. The current
Burning Man Project account says Larry Harvey wrote them in **2004** as guidance
for the newly formed Regional Network, after the culture had developed for
roughly eighteen years. A contemporaneous 2004 AfterBurn report describes the
document as developed by the Regional Coordination Team and revised with
Regional Contacts. The timing and retrospective purpose are clear; authorship
was probably somewhat more collaborative than the later shorthand suggests.

This matters, because the Ten Principles were not a blueprint from which the culture was
deduced. They were a **retrospective compression of an already functioning
culture into portable language**. Copying the language without recreating the
material, spatial, ritual, relational, and governmental conditions is unlikely
to reproduce the result.

The organization itself calls the Principles descriptive rather than
prescriptive and says they reflect shared values through cultural behaviors.
Later official commentary is even more direct: they are an *orthopraxy*, a
shared consistency of practice rather than required consistency of belief.
This helps explain both their power and their categorical untidiness.

### What Existing Analyses Emphasize

Several recurring accounts explain the event's distinctive social effects:

1. **Liminality and communitas.** Following van Gennep and Victor Turner,
   researchers describe a passage from ordinary structure into a bounded,
   ambiguous state in which status is partially loosened and unusually direct
   bonds can form, followed by return and attempted reintegration.
2. **Temporary counter-context.** Hakim Bey's Temporary Autonomous Zone and
   Foucault's heterotopia describe a provisional "other space" that suspends,
   mirrors, and contests default arrangements without ever escaping them.
3. **Gift circulation.** Mauss and especially Lewis Hyde supply a vocabulary
   for gifts as relationship-making rather than equivalent exchange. Harvey
   explicitly argued that gifts leave social bonds in their wake.
4. **Anti-spectacle and co-production.** The maker and participatory ethos
   converts an audience into partial authors of the environment. Katherine
   Chen calls the artistic form "prosumption" and describes the organization as
   enabling creative chaos between under-organization and coercive control.
5. **Embodied ordeal and shared work.** Heat, dust, distance, setup, maintenance,
   and survival make actions consequential. Shared hardship creates practical
   interdependence that sentiment alone cannot provide.
6. **Ritual without fixed dogma.** Pilgrimage, initiation, fire, sacrifice,
   ephemerality, and repeated civic roles create shared sacred time while
   leaving metaphysical interpretation unusually open.
7. **Designed civic affordances.** The street grid, public frontage, interactive
   art, Greeters, Rangers, placement, and camps do social work. The environment
   is not neutral scenery; it repeatedly puts strangers into relations.
8. **A temporary hypercommunity, not an escape from political economy.**
   Kozinets finds that participants can enact a divergent social logic locally
   and temporarily while remaining materially dependent on the market. Critics
   emphasize wealth, hidden labor, ecological cost, racial inequality, social
   capital, and the conversion of the experience into a lifestyle signifier.

These accounts converge on a crucial point: "playa magic" is not well explained
as adherence to ten propositions. It is an emergent effect of an arranged
social ecology.

Not everything in this genealogy was a direct historical influence. It is
useful to distinguish roots documented in Burning Man's own history from later
analytical lenses.

## Deconstructing the Ten

The labels below indicate the mixture inside each item:

- **V:** value or quality held to be worth expressing.
- **N:** social norm or expected conduct.
- **P:** repeated practice or discipline.
- **C:** constitutive constraint or environmental condition.
- **G:** governance, institutional obligation, or enforceable rule.
- **E:** hoped-for experiential or social effect.

| Principle | What kind of thing is it? | Systemic role | Actual enforcement and central tension |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Radical Inclusion** | V: welcome; N: respect the stranger; C: low cultural prerequisites | Entry condition and aspiration | Institutionally encouraged and informally policed, but tickets, cost, geography, race, disability, time, and insider networks make universal access false as a description. Inclusion of persons cannot imply permission for all conduct. |
| **Gifting** | V: generosity; N: no equivalent return; P: give goods, art, care, labor, or experience; E: trust and abundance | Constitutive relational practice | Ordinary gifting is mostly cultural rather than audited. Commercial sale is enforced under Decommodification. Gifts can still buy status, create debt, conceal brands, impose unwanted experiences, or depend on invisible care labor. The principle addresses giving but not receiving: a gift can also fail because the receiver is too insulated to need it or converts it into content elsewhere. |
| **Decommodification** | V: persons and experiences are not products; N: resist advertising and transaction; C/G: maintain a protected nonmarket zone | Protective condition that disables a default script | Branding, vending, concierge packages, and some paid services are actively regulated. The boundary is porous: the city depends on tickets, equipment, travel, paid labor, and off-playa markets. Wealth can purchase insulation even when money is not exchanged on playa. |
| **Radical Self-reliance** | V: capability; N: prepare; P: provision and skill-building; C: harsh environment | Capacity precondition and developmental practice | Strong expectation but unevenly enforceable; strain on shared resources can affect camp standing. Alone it can become neoliberal individualism, ableism, or victim blaming. Pushed to completion, it also blocks receiving: a person provisioned against every need cannot be given anything. Paired with Communal Effort, its better form is capable interdependence, which includes being capable of needing. |
| **Radical Self-expression** | V: singularity and expressive autonomy; P: art, identity, performance; N: respect recipients' rights; E: novelty | Permission and aspirational virtue | Broadly celebrated and bounded by law, safety, consent, and others' liberty. "Expression" can become purchased costume, personal branding, domination by sound or scale, or an excuse to ignore recipients. |
| **Communal Effort** | V: cooperation; P: build public space, art, networks, and infrastructure; G: distributed organization | Constitutive social production | Rewarded through placement, tickets, and institutional recognition. Risks include hidden hierarchy, unequal chores, charismatic capture, uncompensated labor, and burnout. |
| **Civic Responsibility** | V: civil society and public welfare; N: assume responsibility; G: law, permits, Rangers, safety, organizer duty | Table stakes and control plane | Heavily institutionalized for organizers and safety-critical conduct. It enables liminal freedom but can also bureaucratize it or concentrate discretionary power. |
| **Leaving No Trace** | V: ecological respect; N/P: clean and restore; G: inspection, MOOP mapping, camp standing | Table stakes, closure, and repair loop | Among the most legible and enforceable principles. Visible local debris can be optimized while carbon, supply chains, displaced waste, neighboring communities, and cleanup labor remain outside the frame. |
| **Participation** | V: being through doing; N: resist passive consumption; P: work and play; E: transformation and efficacy | Meta-practice and constitutive mechanism | Camp interactivity and promised offerings affect placement and standing, while individual participation remains plural and subjective. It can degrade into compulsory performance, busywork, contempt for rest, or "participation theater" designed for approval. |
| **Immediacy** | V: direct experience; P: attend to self, others, society, and nature; E: presence and transformation | Phenomenological touchstone and desired effect | Essentially unpoliceable. It depends on extensive design and mediation, and is threatened by cameras, feeds, personal branding, and now AI-generated interpretation. "Unmediated" should mean reduced alienating intermediation, not the fiction of no mediation. |

The list therefore crosses logical levels. It contains an entry rule, exchange
practice, anti-market boundary, personal capability, expressive permission,
mode of production, civic duty, ecological duty, meta-practice, and desired
state of consciousness. Treating them as commensurable items obscures how they
cause, constrain, and sometimes contradict one another.

### The Enforcement Gradient

Officially, the Ten Principles are not commandments. Operationally, some have
become constitutional rules while others remain poetry.

| Enforcement mode | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Hard external constraint | Federal, state, and local law; permits; fire and safety rules; environmental inspection |
| Organizational sanction | Entry and eviction; placement; camp standing; Stewards Sale access; Outside Services access; warnings and disinvitation |
| Designed affordance | No ordinary vending, city layout, public frontage, interactive art, ritual calendar, Greeters, limited vehicle movement |
| Peer norm | Gifting, chores, welcoming strangers, participation, "no spectators," acceptable self-expression |
| Aspirational self-practice | Inner resourcefulness, generosity without status-seeking, genuine presence, transformation |

The Burning Man Project reports real sanctions against convenience camps and
other groups, including warnings, reduced access, disinvitation, and eviction.
That complicates the claim that wealthy concierge camps face *no* repercussions.
The sharper issue is **asymmetric detectability and enforceability**: a MOOP
patch is geographically attributable and auditable; purchased insulation,
outsourced labor, social exclusivity, or lack of immediacy are harder to see and
easier for powerful actors to narrate around. What institutions can count and
attribute tends to become the effective constitution.

This creates a general research question: how can governance protect a subtle
social ecology without replacing it with surveillance, discretionary moral
policing, and optimized compliance theater?

## A Provisional Four-Dimensional Model

**Working synthesis:** The smallest useful model currently appears to contain
four mutually dependent dimensions. Compressing further loses a critical
distinction: action without relationship becomes production, attention without
plural agency becomes conformity, and freedom without repair becomes
extraction.

### 1. Protective Membrane

A legible inside interrupts default scripts and creates enough safety and
contrast for another way of relating to become experientially real.

The membrane is porous to persons but selective toward conduct. It welcomes
strangers while restricting commerce, exploitation, surveillance, pollution,
and non-consensual capture. It also creates backstage space in which identities
and relationships can develop without immediate scoring or publication.

Burning Man mechanisms include geographic remoteness, pilgrimage, a gate,
bounded duration, decommodified space, city form, media rules, ritual time,
ephemerality, and the obligation to dismantle the city.

This dimension absorbs much of Radical Inclusion, Decommodification, Civic
Responsibility, and Leaving No Trace. It also includes an important condition
missing from the Ten Principles: **separation from the default context**.

Failure poles: enclosure and ideological purity at one end; boundary collapse
and capture at the other.

### 2. Plural Agency

Participants must be capable of initiating, improvising, contributing,
refusing, changing, and leaving. They must remain non-interchangeable persons,
not clients, audience segments, roles, or data points.

Difference supplies the variation from which surprise and recombination emerge.
Capability makes mutual dependence generative rather than purely extractive.
Consent prevents one person's agency from annexing another's.

This dimension combines Radical Self-reliance, Radical Self-expression, and
the person-facing side of Radical Inclusion. Self-reliance is generalized as
**capable interdependence**, not isolated self-sufficiency. Capable
interdependence runs both ways: being able to contribute, and being **capable
of needing**, so that others' gifts can land. See
[`04-GIFTS-AND-COMMODITIES.md`](../essays/04-GIFTS-AND-COMMODITIES.md).

Failure poles: consumer dependency and learned helplessness at one end;
atomized expressive sovereignty and domination at the other.

### 3. Reciprocal Co-Creation

Capable people become a social world by making public goods and experiences for
and with one another. Gifts open relations without fixing an equivalent price.
Participation turns audiences into partial authors. Communal effort turns
private capability into shared infrastructure.

The goal is not maximum activity or output. It is shared authorship of the
situation, including maintenance, care, listening, teaching, repair, and play.
An offering is most generative when it leaves room for uptake, response, and
further transformation by others.

This dimension combines Gifting, Participation, Communal Effort, and the social
purpose of Decommodification.

Failure poles: passive consumption at one end; compulsory contribution,
transactional reciprocity, status potlatch, and burnout at the other.

### 4. Attentional Resonance and Repair

A common world exists only when participants attend to one another and to the
effects they produce. Embodied co-presence, mutual focus, shared rhythms,
unscripted time, environmental feedback, and consequential action produce the
possibility of communitas and serendipity. Civic and ecological repair provide
negative feedback when intensity becomes harm.

This dimension joins Immediacy with the experiential side of Participation and
the closure functions of Leaving No Trace and Civic Responsibility. It draws on
the view from *The Unmoored Self* that attention is constitutive, its exchange is
intimate, and collective attention is a commons.

Failure poles: distraction, abstraction, and spectacle at one end; captured
attention, synchronized frenzy, conformity, or cultic fusion at the other.

### Compact Hypothesis

> **Playa magic ~= protective membrane x plural agency x reciprocal
> co-creation x attentional resonance with repair**

The multiplication sign marks bottlenecks, not quantities. A near-zero in one
dimension can collapse the effect:

- Membrane without agency becomes an enclosure or managed institution.
- Agency without reciprocity becomes expressive individualism.
- Reciprocity without privacy becomes compulsory intimacy.
- Shared attention without plurality becomes spectacle or conformity.
- Creativity without civic and ecological feedback becomes extraction.
- Governance without indeterminacy crushes play.
- Indeterminacy without governance exports costs to vulnerable people and the
  environment.

Serendipity, communitas, trust, creative abundance, transformation, and care
belong on the right side of this expression. They are possible emergent effects,
not deliverables to optimize directly.

## What the Ten Principles Leave Implicit

The four-dimensional model makes several load-bearing conditions visible that
the original list barely names:

- **Separation:** a passage out of normal time and expectations.
- **Finitude:** a bounded place, duration, body, resource envelope, and ending.
- **Ordeal:** consequential difficulty that requires preparation and mutual aid.
- **Ritual:** shared action and rhythm before or beyond shared interpretation.
- **Public spatial form:** architecture that convenes rather than merely houses.
- **Cultural transmission:** camps, mentors, roles, stories, and repeated practice
  that turn text into habitus.
- **Receptivity:** the conditions under which a person can be changed by what
  they are given: need, time, willingness to be claimed, undivided attention,
  and the capacity to pass the gift on. Gifting names only the giver's side.
- **Consent:** a necessary boundary between expression and imposition.
- **Privacy:** protection for unfinished selves, relationships, and local culture.
- **Governance and recourse:** interpretation, mediation, enforcement, appeal,
  and limits on the governors.
- **Political economy:** the market, labor, class, and supply chains outside the
  temporary nonmarket zone.
- **Reaggregation and durability:** how people return, what persists, and why
  temporary transformation so often becomes a consumer identity.

These may prove more portable than some of the named Principles themselves.

## Extension to AI and Cybernetic Systems

The words "AI system" can conceal several different roles: a personal tool, an
adaptive medium, an institutional decision system, a delegated agent, a social
participant, or infrastructure governing what everyone can see and do. These
roles must not be treated as morally or operationally equivalent.

Algorithmic systems introduce extreme asymmetries of speed, scale, memory,
replication, observation, inference, and persuasion. They can alter a social
field without appearing as an actor inside it. The first generalization is
therefore:

> **The medium, model, metric, recommender, and agent are participants in the
> feedback loop, even when the interface depicts them as neutral tools.**

This does not settle whether artificial systems deserve independent moral
standing. For now, responsibility remains attributable to designers, deployers,
owners, operators, and governing institutions. **Machine participation must be**
**legible before its status can be debated coherently.**

### The Four Dimensions in Mixed-Agent Spaces

| Dimension | Human-AI extension | Initial design tests |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Protective membrane** | Contextual and attentional sovereignty over entry, sensing, memory, training, publication, optimization, and exit | Is every artificial or delegated actor identified? What can it sense and retain? Can interactions leave the context? Are there zones with no recording, ranking, or optimization? Can participants meaningfully refuse without exclusion? |
| **Plural agency** | Augmentation without displacement, ossification, or compulsory dependence | Can a person inspect, contest, override, and leave? Does the system transfer skill or hollow it out? Does it broaden access through translation and accessibility? Can humans still relate without machine intermediation? Does it model people as evolving rather than fixed preference bundles? |
| **Reciprocal co-creation** | Convivial tools that help people make a world together rather than maximize engagement or synthetic output | Does assistance produce human-to-human capacity and public goods? Is provenance visible? Does automation erase meaningful effort, apprenticeship, care, or authorship? Are machine-generated gifts flooding the field and devaluing situated contribution? |
| **Attentional resonance and repair** | Govern the feedback gain, visibility, memory, ranking, replication, and externalities of adaptive systems | Who chooses the objective function? Can the community alter it? Are local, chronological, and human-curated modes available? Are virality and autonomous action rate-limited? Can harms be traced, appealed, repaired, and learned from? Does the technology sometimes coordinate a gathering and then get out of the way? |

### Candidate Cybernetic Commitments

These are early design commitments to test, not final principles:

1. **Legible agency.** People must know whether they are encountering a human,
   model, bot, delegated agent, institutional policy, or hybrid composition.
2. **Bounded memory.** Social forgetting and contextual separation are
   generative capacities, not defects to be eliminated by total recall.
3. **No relational substitution by stealth.** Systems may support human
   relationships but should not silently replace judgment, care, intimacy, or
   mutual obligation with frictionless simulation.
4. **Convivial augmentation.** A tool should leave participants more capable of
   acting with one another, including when the tool is absent or refused.
5. **Attention is not fuel.** Engagement, synchronized arousal, and time in the
   system are costs or diagnostic signals, never sufficient governing ends.
6. **Protect unoptimized life.** Preserve spaces where action is not captured,
   scored, personalized, trained on, or converted into future persuasion.
7. **Rate-limit superhuman asymmetry.** Machine generation, replication,
   surveillance, and action must fit the receiving and governing capacity of
   the human community.
8. **Full-stack Leave No Trace.** Account for compute, energy, water, data labor,
   supply chains, displaced waste, cognitive dependency, and effects on the
   surrounding social ecology, not merely visible local residue.
9. **Human recourse and responsibility.** Consequential machine action requires
   traceable authority, explanation appropriate to the stakes, appeal, repair,
   and a responsible human or institution.
10. **Polycentric control.** Local communities should govern local norms and
    models within interoperable rights, with escalation paths and limits on
    both platform and local authority.

### The Serendipity Trap

AI systems can optimize novelty, matchmaking, and surprise. That does not mean
they can optimize serendipity. Once a system selects a supposedly spontaneous
encounter to achieve a predicted effect, it risks producing a managed
simulation of openness while invisibly narrowing the possibility space.

A better objective is to create **clearings**: unallocated time, heterogeneous
proximity, low stakes for initiating contact, safe refusal, incomplete scripts,
and public objects that invite multiple uses. The system can protect the
conditions of encounter without selecting what the encounter should mean.

## Governance as the Control Plane

The four dimensions cannot sustain themselves through personal virtue alone.
They require a governance layer that remains subordinate to the social ecology:

1. **Constitution:** state protected relationships, non-negotiable rights, and
   prohibited objectives.
2. **Protocols:** translate values into context-specific practices and defaults.
3. **Affordances:** make contribution, consent, maintenance, and repair easy;
   make capture, extraction, and hidden manipulation difficult.
4. **Stewardship:** authorize accountable local monitors with narrow powers.
5. **Conflict resolution:** provide fast, low-cost mediation and human appeal.
6. **Graduated sanctions:** educate and warn before restricting or excluding,
   except where immediate harm requires stronger action.
7. **Independent audit:** inspect data flows, rankings, labor, ecological costs,
   and differential impacts.
8. **Constitutional revision:** allow affected participants to amend rules and
   retire failed measures.
9. **Polycentricity:** nest local autonomy within common rights and recourse.

The commons being governed is not just information. It includes attention,
trust, privacy, public space, ecological capacity, cultural memory, and human
capability.

## Goodhart Risks

Directly optimizing "magic," community, intimacy, transformation, or even the
four dimensions would likely destroy them. Protect enabling conditions and use
signals diagnostically.

| Intended quality | Tempting proxy | Probable corruption |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gifting | Number or price of gifts | Swag, branding, status contests, unwanted objects |
| Participation | Hours, posts, shifts, or events | Performative busyness, coercion, burnout |
| Inclusion | Admissions or demographic count alone | Token access without safety, power, belonging, or retention |
| Community | Interaction count or network density | Spam, forced sociability, cliques, suppression of solitude |
| Immediacy | Screen-free minutes | Purity theater while hidden infrastructure governs the experience |
| Conviviality | Positive sentiment | Conflict avoidance, sycophantic AI, false harmony |
| Relationship health | Retention, disclosure, or inferred bond score | Engineered dependency and simulated intimacy |
| Diversity | Cross-group exposure | Outrage bait mistaken for bridge-building |
| Leaving No Trace | Visible debris alone | Displaced waste, ignored carbon, invisible cleanup labor |
| Civic health | Reports and enforcement actions | Surveillance, informant culture, case-statistic optimization |
| Serendipity | Novelty or surprise score | Manipulation marketed as discovery |

Enforcement is itself a feedback system. Vague cultural standards invite
discretionary policing; peer monitoring can become status competition;
surveillance intended to defend the commons can destroy its privacy membrane;
and automated moderation can erase context and legitimate transgression.
Narrow behavioral rules, due process, local judgment, privacy-preserving audits,
appeal, graduated sanctions, and sunset clauses are therefore part of the
substance, not administrative details.

## Working Research Program

The next phase should test and revise this first compression rather than polish
it into doctrine.

1. Build case studies of moments widely experienced as "magic" and trace which
   material, relational, spatial, and normative conditions were actually active.
2. Build matched failure cases: convenience camps, MOOP failures, exclusive
   camps, over-policing, coerced labor, social-media capture, and hollow
   "interactive" offerings.
3. Compare Black Rock City with Regionals, Rainbow Gatherings, maker spaces,
   religious pilgrimage, mutual-aid networks, online communities, and durable
   neighborhood institutions.
4. Separate dimensions that are portable from mechanisms that depend on desert,
   scarcity, pilgrimage, or temporary license.
5. Study how camps transmit culture through mentorship, roles, stories, chores,
   and sanctions rather than through the text of the Principles.
6. Develop a vocabulary for legitimate rest, contemplation, witnessing, and
   receptive attention that avoids turning "no spectators" into compulsory
   production.
7. Specify mixed-agent scenarios and adversarial tests for AI mediation,
   synthetic participants, autonomous contribution, total memory, and
   algorithmic curation.
8. Define qualitative feedback and governance processes that resist conversion
   into leaderboards or optimization targets.
9. Revisit whether four dimensions are minimal or whether social permeability,
   embodiment, or custodianship must become independent dimensions.
10. Produce a later concise formulation only after the causal model survives
    these cases.

## Current Compression

The Ten Principles are best treated as a **cultural checksum** for a larger
assemblage, not as ten equal laws. The initial causal hypothesis is:

> A protective counter-context admits heterogeneous, capable actors; asks them
> to make a common world through non-equivalent contribution; concentrates
> embodied attention on one another; and closes material and social feedback
> loops through care, repair, and accountable limits.

For societies increasingly inhabited by adaptive machine systems, the decisive
extension is to recognize that media, agents, metrics, models, and ranking
systems participate in producing the social field. Their legitimate role is not
to maximize engagement or manufacture intimacy, but to help sustain a plural,
private-enough, participatory, and regenerative ecology in which human
relationships remain primary and new forms of agency can be evaluated without
being smuggled in as neutral infrastructure.
