# Permeable, Not Purchasable

## Repairing Participation in a Connected Black Rock City

This essay applies the four-dimensional model in [`BEYOND-TEN-PRINCIPLES.md`](../research/BEYOND-TEN-PRINCIPLES.md) to convenience culture, affluent
spectatorship, EDM consumption, influencer capture, and ubiquitous satellite connectivity. Its proposals are working synthesis, not current Burning Man policy.

## Diagnose Conduct, Not Class

Black Rock City's cultural problem is often described as rich people ruining Burning Man. That diagnosis is morally crude and institutionally weak. Wealth can finance difficult art, subsidize other participants, and give organizers
time to build ambitious gifts. An RV can be an exclusionary wall or an accessibility necessity. A flight can be status theater or the only feasible route for an international participant, elder, disabled person, or someone with
care obligations. Dancing at a sound camp can be receptive participation rather than passive consumption.

The relevant distinction is not rich versus poor. It is:

> **Reciprocal participation versus purchased exemption.**

Does money, logistics, celebrity, imaging, or connectivity help a person contribute? Or does it insulate them from dependence, preparation, chores, consent, contingency, and encounter while other people provide the experience?

The demographic and economic shift is still relevant. The representative 2025 BRC Census estimated that 47.6 percent of participants had personal incomes of at least $100,000, compared with 23.9 percent in 2015; 10.8 percent reported at least $300,000. Cost was the most commonly reported obstacle to attendance, followed by difficulty getting time away from work. These data cannot say who Burned well. They show that formal openness coexists with a steep selection gradient.

The response should use all four dimensions: repair the membrane, preserve plural agency, make reciprocity unavoidable but not uniform, and govern the attention loops introduced by sound, cameras, platforms, and Starlink.

## 1. What Resources May Buy—and What They May Not

The [overview](../README.md) gives the broader motivation: different
ways of relating need practical conditions. Here the question is which
advantages may become which privileges. Money can support a camp without
buying exemption from responsibility. Hospitality can welcome someone without
granting permission to record. Technical access can make a service possible
without giving its operator authority over every later use.

These are distinct permissions, not a single package called participation.
For an AI artwork, contributing a prompt might authorize a local response;
retaining it, publishing it, training on it, or acting for its author each
needs a separate basis. A sponsor's payment does not supply the missing
permission. Nor can a participant authorize use of every bystander's likeness.

The practical test is: **what did this contribution or encounter authorize,
who could grant that authority, and what remains outside it?** This extends
decommodification into decisions collaborators can make without adopting a
specialized vocabulary.


A protective membrane does not keep wealth or technology out. It admits useful
resources while filtering default-world contracts, status systems,
surveillance, and promotional logic.

Decommodification already describes this kind of selective boundary. It resists
the substitution of consumption for participation, not the existence of money
outside the city. Existing policy prohibits packaged tours and convenience
camps in which a large payment purchases relief from building, cooking,
interactivity, strike, or Leave No Trace. Outside Services providers require
authorization and remain limited in what they may deliver.

Enforcement is more substantial than the familiar claim that the organization
does nothing. Burning Man reports warnings, adverse standing, reduced Stewards
Sale access, lost placement, lost Outside Services access, temporary
disinvitation, and permanent eviction. Yet the process is difficult to evaluate
from outside. Investigations are often complaint-driven, and published figures
rarely expose denominators, evidence standards, reversals, or equity effects.

More importantly, policy can target the wrong proxy. Burning Man prohibited
vendor-delivered housing in an effort to reduce RV walls and convenience camps,
then concluded that the ban had limited cultural effect while increasing cost
and burden for international participants and people unable to transport or
safely drive large vehicles. Some delivered housing returned in 2025. The
lesson is not that regulation failed. It is that the social harm was
**insulation**, while delivered housing was only an unreliable indicator.

Architecture could offer a more direct intervention:

- Limit long stretches of blank RV, container, or service frontage.
- Require visible pedestrian entrances and public-facing shade on major streets.
- Put private infrastructure inside camp plans rather than around their edges.
- Preserve genuinely private and quiet space without allowing gated compounds.
- Evaluate whether conveniences serve access, safety, or public contribution,
  not whether they look austere.

This need not require disclosure of disability or private hardship. Universal
design and confidential accommodation are better than demanding that people
perform deservingness.

The membrane should be **porous to resources but resistant to exemption**. A
generator that powers public art and accessible cooling crosses differently
from a generator that sustains a sealed hospitality enclave, even if the
machines are identical.

Purchased exemption may also diminish opportunities to **receive**. When
services replace every encounter in which someone might ask a neighbor for
help, the participant loses occasions for unplanned reciprocity. This is a
hypothesis about arrangements, not a diagnosis of a comfortable person's
inner life. Material provision does not make every gift redundant; ordeal
does not guarantee receptivity. The design goal is accessible opportunities
to depend on and contribute to others, without manufacturing deprivation or
requiring participants to prove that an encounter changed them. See
[`04-GIFTS-AND-COMMODITIES.md`](04-GIFTS-AND-COMMODITIES.md).

## 2. Plural Agency, Not One Correct Burn

Repair cannot impose one physically demanding model of legitimate
participation. Elders, disabled people, parents, international travelers, people
with limited leave, and participants doing emotional, administrative,
translation, or care work need different affordances.  This is especially important as the "OG burners" from the 1990s and 2000s are an aging population, and will require more accomodation over time in order for BRC to continue absorbing their perspectives and wisdom.

BRC already contains examples of convenience that increases agency: Ticket Aid,
accessibility vehicles, Burner Express, luggage and water options, expedited
collective entry, and dedicated camping. A bus is easier than driving, but it
also reduces vehicles and can broaden access. The relevant question is what
capacity the convenience creates and what dependency it displaces.

The airport is similarly over-symbolized. Only about 0.7 percent of 2025 Census
respondents entered through Black Rock City Airport, compared with 10.8 percent
by Burner Express Bus and 85 percent in other vehicles. Flying is a potent image
of hierarchy but a small route numerically. A categorical flight ban would
punish some international, time-constrained, operational, and disabled
participants while leaving the deeper market for exemption intact.

Better interventions would include:

- Publish aggregate aviation use and full environmental cost.
- Price or offset aviation externalities without selling moral absolution.
- Prohibit airport-linked concierge packages and private hospitality transfer.
- Insert an arrival threshold between passenger and citizen.
- Offer a short menu of first contributions at bus and air arrival commons.

An arrival commons could teach neighborhood norms, match a participant to an
accessible volunteer shift, invite help unloading common equipment, or offer a
repair and survival skill. The point is not compulsory labor at the gate. It is
ritual transition: one arrives as a transported passenger and enters as someone
with agency in a common world.

Greeters already run a version of this threshold. "Welcome home," the dust
angel, and the bell are gifts a newcomer is made to receive and must accept by
taking part. The Gate admits; the Greeters teach reception. An arrival commons
would extend a working practice rather than invent one.

Participation is not only production. Listening, grieving, witnessing,
dancing, accepting care, and resting are participation when they are a way of
*receiving* what others offer. Receiving differs from consuming in what it
does to both parties: the giver is attended to as a particular person, and the
receiver allows the gift to change them and later passes something on. A
model of participation that counts only building and performing would exclude
many elders, disabled people, and exhausted workers, and would miss half of
the gift economy. The failure begins when a person treats builders, workers,
performers, neighbors, and photographed strangers as replaceable
infrastructure for their private experience: consuming without receiving.
[`04-GIFTS-AND-COMMODITIES.md`](04-GIFTS-AND-COMMODITIES.md) develops the
conditions of receptivity.

## 3. Reciprocal Co-Creation Beyond the Headliner

Burning Man does not centrally book performers or issue an official lineup.
Sound camps and DJs are participants who create a major art form. EDM is not the
enemy of participation. The problem is importing the producer-audience
relationship of the commercial festival: celebrity names drive demand,
backstage access manufactures status, and an optimized crowd receives a product.

In 2015, Burning Man asked camps not to promote celebrity DJ lineups far in
advance because doing so recast the city as an EDM product. That request
correctly targeted promotion rather than dance music.

The next step is to create **participation ramps** around high-attention
offerings:

- Open-deck sessions and collaborative music-making.
- Dance instruction and forms friendly to varied bodies.
- Beginner roles in lighting, stagecraft, safety, hospitality, and strike.
- Sober and low-sensory participation spaces.
- Visible credit for maintenance and teardown, not just performers.
- No wristband economy for public offerings or status markets around access.

Famous artists should remain free to perform. Camps should not use fame to
reconstruct a commercial hierarchy whose currency is proximity rather than
money.

Placement already evaluates interactivity, decommodification, strain on shared
resources, and whether camps delivered what they proposed. Evaluation could ask
whether newcomers became collaborators, undesirable work was fairly governed,
neighbors experienced the camp as porous, and camp members could safely refuse
or report mistreatment.

Raw attendance, event counts, volunteer hours, and frontage size are poor
proxies. They invite token workshops, fictitious shifts, coerced labor, and
interactive theater designed for Placement rather than participants. Better
evidence is plural and qualitative: confidential member feedback, neighbor
testimony, occasional observation, worker reports, and multi-year patterns.

Excellent food, showers, professional fabrication, and air conditioning are not
intrinsically corrupting. Ask instead:

- Who governs and maintains the resource?
- Who bears the undesirable work?
- Can workers report abuse without retaliation?
- Can money excuse a member from all reciprocal responsibility?
- Does private comfort support public contribution or replace it?

No one should have to suffer equally. No one should be able to purchase
citizenship as a service performed by subordinates.

## 4. Attention, Images, and Status Capture

Photography can deepen attention. It is art, memory, documentation, and one way
ephemeral work survives. The target should be exploitative capture, not cameras
or follower counts.

Current rules already require consent before photographing or recording people.
Non-personal, promotional, editorial, and commercial projects require
registration; credentialed equipment carries identifiers; brand use is
prohibited; public livestream proposals are not approved; and drones are
strictly regulated. The Temple applies an especially strong consent standard,
including people in the background.

Influencer practice becomes extractive when the playa, artwork, and people are
converted into raw material for status accumulation in another context. The
subject cannot know the eventual audience, ranking system, model training use,
or commercial association. A moment governed by local norms is silently moved
into a global attention market.

The flow also runs inward. The anticipated audience of followers becomes the
mediator of the capturer's desire, in René Girard's sense: the experience is
valued for what followers will want. Those expectations flow back through the
phone onto the playa. They change behavior (posing, queuing, re-staging),
create norms around "photo op" spots at particular artworks, and shape the art
itself toward works made to be photographed rather than experienced. Funding
that depends on compelling images can push in the same direction without any
influencer involved. [`04-GIFTS-AND-COMMODITIES.md`](04-GIFTS-AND-COMMODITIES.md)
develops this outward diversion and inward counterflow.

Follower count is not a reliable definition of this harm. It can overcapture
artists, activists, educators, journalists, and marginalized storytellers.
Enforcement should prioritize conduct:

- Sponsorship or undisclosed brand placement.
- Unconsented subjects or vulnerable bystanders.
- Livestreaming and posting that collapse contextual boundaries.
- Refusal to remove material after consent is withdrawn or a violation shown.
- Misrepresentation of participant work as the recorder's own production.

Positive affordances can make consensual media easier:

- Portrait areas with controlled backgrounds and explicit participation.
- Camera hosts who explain consent and watch frame boundaries.
- Easy on-playa registration and visible identification for larger projects.
- Practical review, deletion, attribution, and takedown pathways.
- Camera-free sanctuaries where the presumption is no capture.

Badges can communicate preference but must never become blanket consent. Consent
is specific, situated, and revocable. The protective membrane must extend
beyond the shutter into distribution, monetization, and machine reuse.

## Starlink and the Collapse of Distance

Starlink changes the gradient rather than introducing connectivity from
nothing. BRC operations already use networks; participant camps can connect to
a city-wide backbone with compatible equipment; and emergency conditions have
led Burning Man to provide public Wi-Fi, charging, mobile towers, weather
updates, family contact, and Exodus coordination.

Connectivity has legitimate uses:

- Emergency and operational communication.
- Assistive communication and telehealth.
- Caregiving and proof-of-life obligations.
- Weather, transportation, and changed travel plans.
- Coordination for art, camps, and accessibility.

It also changes the phenomenology of the city. A difficult message no longer
waits for re-entry. Work can follow the participant into camp. Every encounter
can be posted into a status loop before it has been metabolized. People sitting
together can inhabit separate algorithmic contexts. The default world's
audience becomes an invisible participant on playa.

A ban would be difficult to enforce without searches or a device registry and
would remove real capability. A better design distinguishes three layers:

| Layer | Legitimate purpose | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Operational and emergency | Gate, Rangers, medical, weather, transport, safety | Reliable, protected, and not repurposed for participant surveillance |
| Care and logistics commons | Family contact, accessibility, telehealth, changed travel, proof of life | Universally available at known places and times; minimal logging; no hardship test |
| Private participant networks | Camp operations and discretionary access | Kept away from public frontage; no public livestreams; transparent privacy practice; culturally bounded use |

Camps could adopt an **attention-respecting network** covenant:

- Put screens and workstations away from public interaction space.
- Publish connection hours rather than provide ambient always-on Wi-Fi.
- Minimize logs and do not inspect content.
- Prohibit default livestreaming and background capture.
- Offer device lockers, offline meals, or periods of collective disconnection.
- Make emergency and accessibility exceptions ordinary rather than stigmatized.

These measures are intentionally modest. Mandated disconnection can become
purity performance or coercion. The purpose is to restore a temporal membrane,
not to turn analog hardship into a status competition.

## Repair, Sanctions, and Anti-Goodhart Governance

Enforcement should repair the social field rather than maximize case counts.
First violations ordinarily call for conversation, deletion or takedown,
restitution, training, and a remediation plan. Repeated deception, paid
exemption, retaliation, commercial exploitation, or deliberate consent
violations justify stronger loss of placement, tickets, credentials, service
access, or ultimately admission.

People harmed by imagery need practical takedown help. Artists need attribution
or removal. Workers need confidential anti-retaliation channels. Neighbors need
mediation. A camp should repair damaged relationships, not merely improve a
score.

Ostrom's commons work suggests clear boundaries, participant rule-making,
accountable monitoring, low-cost conflict resolution, and graduated sanctions.
Goodhart's warning requires that these never become a public participation
leaderboard.

Useful safeguards include:

- Publish anonymized enforcement statistics, outcomes, appeals, and equity
  effects.
- Audit samples and credible complaints rather than surveil everyone.
- Use several changing qualitative signals rather than one fixed metric.
- Evaluate patterns over multiple years while allowing genuine remediation.
- Separate acculturation, investigation, adjudication, and appeal roles.
- Sunset rules that do not improve the four underlying dimensions.

## Applying These Choices to AI

An AI agreement should address the same conversions as a camp agreement:
resources into authority, access into recording, contribution into ownership,
and local recognition into standing elsewhere. A system can help translate,
coordinate, or make art while remaining limited in what those tasks authorize.

For a proposed installation, identify who can approve each subsequent use,
who can refuse without losing unrelated camp benefits, who can pause disputed
activity, and who must repair its consequences. Apply those limits to the
operator and sponsors as well as the model. A consent screen cannot supply
powers its designer never had.

[The worked encounter in the AI brief](../AI-CONSTITUTIONS.md#start-with-an-encounter)
shows how to translate this into an agreement. The purpose is to leave the
creative outcome open while making the consequential permissions explicit.

## A Portfolio of Interventions

| Problem | Membrane | Agency | Co-creation | Attention and repair |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sealed luxury camps | Porous frontage and no concierge packaging | Confidential accommodation and varied roles | Shared governance of chores and resources | Neighbor feedback, worker recourse, graduated sanctions |
| Fly-in spectatorship | No airport-linked hospitality market | Arrival commons and accessible contribution ramps | Transition from passenger to citizen | Publish environmental cost and support re-entry |
| Headliner rave dynamics | No advance status marketing or wristband economy | Open roles around sound production | Workshops, open decks, strike, and shared craft | Sound zoning, low-sensory alternatives, qualitative review |
| Influencer capture | Contextual media boundaries | Consent, refusal, attribution, deletion | Consensual media-making as a gift | No-capture sanctuaries and takedown support |
| Ambient Starlink | Layered network zones | Universal care access and opt-out | Connectivity used for coordination, not substitution | Bounded hours, minimal logs, offline rhythms |

No single row is a solution. The dimensions constrain one another. A rule that
strengthens the membrane but excludes disabled participants weakens plural
agency. A contribution requirement that reduces spectatorship but coerces labor
destroys reciprocity. Surveillance that catches influencers can colonize the
privacy it claims to defend.

## Closing Reflection

Black Rock City cannot keep the default world outside. Wealth, aviation,
electronic music, cameras, networks, and algorithmic audiences will cross the
boundary. The viable task is to metabolize them:

- Turn resources into shared capability.
- Turn arrivals into citizenship.
- Turn audiences into collaborators and witnesses.
- Turn images into consensual memory.
- Turn networks into bounded care infrastructure.
- Turn violations into repair where possible and exclusion where necessary.

The membrane succeeds not when participation is equally uncomfortable, but when
no one can purchase exemption from reciprocity. Presence is not the absence of
technology. It is a social achievement in which the people and place before us
have a stronger claim on our attention than the distant systems trying to turn
the moment into content.
