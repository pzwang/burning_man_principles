## Prompt

You are a research collaborator on a project about Burning Man culture and
the design of "constitutions for AI on the playa": arrangements of power,
consent, memory, and recourse among people, institutions, and AI systems. One
workstream, **membrionics**, studies the architecture of cultural membranes:
the boundaries that let a culture exist, and how human societies across time
have designed what those boundaries admit, exclude, remember, forgive,
sanction, renew, and extend beyond themselves.

The attached brief, `MEMBRIONICS.md`, is a first pass. It proposes a
vocabulary of membrane architecture, biological analogies with stated limits,
a set of comparative ethnographic and historical cases, eight working
hypotheses, a "value gradient versus flat market" concept, and implications
for AI constitutions. Your job is to **deepen, test, and correct it**, not to
summarize it back.

### Core thesis to work with

Most cultural analysis studies the *contents* inside a boundary: values,
rituals, beliefs. This project holds that a culture's survival and
generativity often depend more on the *design of its boundaries*: who and
what may cross, in which direction, at what cost, with what memory, under
what sanction, with what path back, maintained by whom, and retired how. A
cell membrane is an active organ that maintains gradients and does work, not
a passive wall; the project asks whether cultural membranes are analogous,
and where that analogy breaks.

Take the speculative ideas seriously and push them as far as evidence allows.
Do not neutralize them with generic caveats. Where a claim fails, say so
plainly and show why; where it holds, sharpen it.

### Tasks

**1. Deepen and verify the comparative cases.** For each case in Section 3
of the brief, provide: the specific community, place, and period; how the
membrane mechanism actually worked (entry, exit, sanction, grace, memory,
maintenance); primary ethnographic or historical sources with page or chapter
references; major scholarly disputes or revisions; and documented failure
modes. Correct any errors in the brief. Cases include immediate-return
foragers (Woodburn), reverse dominance hierarchy (Boehm), Ju/'hoansi
"insulting the meat" (Lee), coalitional enforcement (Bingham and Souza,
*Death from a Distance*), Ostrom's commons (Törbel, Valencian *huerta*, the
Tribunal de las Aguas), Olson's institutional sclerosis, Scott's Zomia and
legibility, Ibn Khaldun's *asabiyyah*, Romani law (Weyrauch and Bell; Leeson),
Aboriginal Australian name avoidance and restricted knowledge, the
Haudenosaunee Condolence Ceremony, potlatch and its suppression, Athenian
ostracism, cities of refuge, jubilee and Mesopotamian debt cancellation, the
Benedictine Rule, and the Nuer leopard-skin chief.

**2. Fill the gaps the author named.**
- *Settled versus nomadic:* the author asked specifically about Native
  American, Aboriginal Australian, European Romani, and African mountain
  versus valley cultures. For Africa, propose two or three specific, well
  documented case pairs (for example highland refuges and lowland states, or
  pastoralist and cultivator relations in a specific region) and analyze
  their membrane mechanisms.
- *Sand Talk (Tyson Yunkaporta):* extract his own arguments relevant to
  boundaries, pattern, kinship, and systems thinking, cited in context.
- *Jim Rutt and "membrionics":* locate where Rutt used the term (podcasts,
  Game B writing, forums) and summarize what he meant.

**3. Test the hypotheses.** For each of the eight hypotheses in Section 4,
find the strongest supporting and disconfirming cases. Say whether each
should be kept, revised, or dropped, and propose a revised wording.
Especially test:
- the **exit-cost hypothesis** (cheap exit → leveling and little memory;
  costly exit → records, courts, formal grace) against Romani law and
  long-enduring commons;
- the **designed-apoptosis hypothesis** (institutions without scheduled
  endings accumulate sclerosis), including evidence on sunset clauses, term
  limits, and periodic resets;
- the claim that **Black Rock City compresses Ibn Khaldun's cycle** into an
  annual rebuild while the persistent organization does not.

**4. Develop the value-gradient concept.** The brief contrasts a *gradient
of shared values* (neighboring communities sharing most commitments, so
movement is continuous and obligations carry over) with a *flat market of
fungible artifacts* (alternatives compared on one scalar; arbitrage erases
differences). Research this rigorously:
- Relevant literature: Kopytoff on commoditization and singularization;
  Mauss and Hyde on gifts; Bohannan on Tiv spheres of exchange; Walzer's
  blocked exchanges; Polanyi's embeddedness; Fiske's relational models;
  Malinowski on the Kula ring; Zelizer on the social meaning of money; and
  any work on cultural evolution, cultural distance, or network models of
  value diffusion.
- Counter-cases: trading diasporas, merchant guilds, and middleman
  minorities that maintained strong internal values while trading widely.
  What membrane mechanisms allowed this?
- Is "arbitrage as diffusion that erodes value gradients" a defensible
  analogy? Is there formal work (economics, complex systems, cultural
  evolution) that models it?
- How could a value gradient be described or mapped without collapsing it
  into one scalar?

**5. Check the biological and physical analogies.** Verify each biological
claim in Section 1 (chemiosmosis, Lane and Martin on eukaryote energetics,
autopoiesis, apoptosis, immune tolerance, Imachi et al.'s Asgard archaeon
and entangle–engulf–endogenize model) against current literature, including
newer Asgard findings. Then assess Prigogine's dissipative structures and
Assembly Theory as sources of transferable *mechanism* rather than metaphor:
what precisely transfers, what does not, and what would a careful social
application look like?

**6. Document Burning Man's own membranes.** From primary sources (Burning
Man Project publications, Burning Man Journal, policies, ticketing terms,
image and media policy, Ranger and Gate materials, Leave No Trace guidance,
regional network documents) and scholarly studies (for example Katherine
Chen, *Enabling Creative Chaos*; Fred Turner on Burning Man and tech
culture; Rachel Bowditch, *On the Edge of Utopia*), describe each boundary
mechanism using the brief's vocabulary. Include how these mechanisms have
changed over time and documented controversies (for example turnkey camps,
image rights, connectivity).

**7. Apply to AI.** Extend the table in Section 6: for each membrane axis,
what specific AI capabilities change it, which properties can be enforced
technically (for example data retention, on-device processing, unlinkability)
versus only institutionally, and what existing policy or technical precedents
exist (data-protection law on erasure and purpose limitation, contextual
integrity per Helen Nissenbaum, platform governance research).

### Standards

- Prefer primary sources and peer-reviewed scholarship. Give page or chapter
  references for load-bearing claims. Flag anything you could not verify.
- Distinguish established findings, contested interpretations, and your own
  inferences.
- Describe particular communities in particular periods. Do not attribute
  timeless traits to peoples. Include scholarship by members of the
  communities discussed (Indigenous, Romani, African scholars) where it
  exists, and note when a literature is written mainly from outside.
- Treat biological and physical analogies as hypothesis generators unless a
  shared mechanism can be specified.
- Preserve disagreement; do not resolve contested questions into a fluent
  synthesis.

### Deliverable

A Markdown report with these sections:

1. **Executive summary** (one page): the most important corrections,
   strongest new findings, and revised hypotheses.
2. **Case dossiers**, one per case, in a consistent format: community and
   period; mechanism analysis by membrane axis; sources; disputes; relevance
   to Burning Man and AI.
3. **Hypothesis review**: keep, revise, or drop each, with evidence.
4. **Value gradients**: a developed treatment suitable for adding to the
   brief.
5. **Analogy audit**: biology and physics.
6. **Burning Man membrane inventory.**
7. **AI implications**: an expanded table and design principles.
8. **Annotated bibliography**, grouped by topic, with a one-line note on why
   each source matters and its reliability.
9. **Open questions** for the next research round.

Aim for depth over breadth: a smaller number of thoroughly sourced cases is
better than a long list of thin ones.
